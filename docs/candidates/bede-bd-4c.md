# Bede BD-4C (reference)

Reference design, added 2026-10-29 at Bill's request (AER-4145): "they belong in
the review much like the Cozy aircraft." The BD-4C is a kit in production, not a
revival candidate. It is compared with the targets and with the Cozy MKIV like
for like, and it is not ranked. Written by hand in the dossier shape, not
generated from `docs/longlist/candidates.json`. Nobody has been contacted.

Three airplanes share the name, and every number below says which one it
belongs to:
- **BD-4** (1968 on): the original kit, built in hundreds. It has a
  panel-rib wing on a tubular spar and a 42 in cabin (BD-4B sheet). It is the
  source of the fleet weights and the accident record.
- **BD-4B**: the later production standard of the same airframe (Bedecorp
  sheet, 2013).
- **BD-4C** (debuted 2009): 46 in cabin, 14 in longer fuselage, a new bonded
  wet wing with aluminium honeycomb ribs, S-ply spring main gear, and a
  castering nose wheel. Six on the FAA registry.

The older BD-4 has one measured record, which does not translate to the
BD-4C: in the CAFE 400 race, Roger Mellema's 180 hp BD-4 won the experimental
three-or-more-seat class in 1983 and in 1984, carrying 800 lb in four seats at
2,350 lb gross. It averaged 163.0 mph at 21.70 mpg (1983) and 161.6 mph at
23.10 mpg (1984). These are course averages over a 400-mile closed course
with climbs to about 7,300 ft, not level cruise (R; CAFE results, read from
the scanned pages, [Wayback index](https://web.archive.org/web/20240521224954/https://cafe.foundation/v2/research_cafe400.php)).

## Screen (published numbers; like for like)

Useful load and range are targets: a miss is reported with its size. Cruise above 160 KTAS is a floor.

- **As published (Bede's sheet)**
  - Useful load: 1,150 lb, 15% over the target.
  - Range: 782-892 nm, 11-22% short. Bede gives no power, fuel or reserve.
  - Cruise: 159 KTAS at 75% on 180 hp, 1 kt under the floor; 167 KTAS on
    200 hp, over the floor and under the preferred 180. Neither states an
    altitude.
  - Payload at 1,000 nm: none on the published range.
- **Measured or reported (N2468Z, builder)**
  - Useful load: 1,119 lb, 12% over.
  - Range: 684-717 nm at 65% on 48 gal, 28-32% short, reserve not stated.
  - Cruise: 133.5 KTAS at 75% and 4,500 ft, before fairings; 142 KTAS at 65%,
    no fairings. Both are under the floor.
- **Aeromomentum fit (first cut):** yes. Bede recommends 180-210 hp, and the
  AM20T's assumed 195 hp continuous sits inside that band.

Sanity flags:

- the 180 and 200 hp weights are the BD-4/BD-4B sheet's, carried into the BD-4C sheet; the one BD-4C weight on record (N2468Z) is 31 lb over them, a smaller factory-to-built gap than the Cozy's 129 lb, on one aircraft
- no published speed states an altitude, and none states a weight or fuel flow, so none can calibrate a drag model
- the published "max range" fits 51 gal with no reserve at economy power (our check below), not the 80 gal option
- the BD-4C sheet's cruise speeds are 9-15 mph lower than the 2013 BD-4B sheet's at the same power, and its stall speeds are 12-14 mph higher. The newer sheet is the more believable of the two

## Why it died

Not dead. The BD-4 has been sold since 1969, and the BD-4C since 2009.

## Idea ledger (what a new airplane should keep; AERO's judgement)

- **A fuselage that needs no jig and no welder.** Pre-formed aluminium
  angles and precut gussets bolt into a truss that defines its own geometry,
  and flat sheet is bonded to it. It is the shortest build in this survey
  (about 700 h, F) for a four-seater. The one BD-4C weight on record sits
  31 lb over the factory figure.
- **A wet wing built from factory-made ribs on a tube.** CNC-cut honeycomb
  ribs and a tube spar come from the kit. The builder bonds the skins and
  seals the tank bays, and chooses 51-80 gal by how many bays to seal.
- **Removable wings by design.** The panels sleeve over the centre-section
  spar and bolt on. The cost is a constant-chord, AR 6.6 wing with fixed gear
  and a box fuselage: our best L/D is 12.3, against the Cozy's 16.3.

## Safety record

The record is the BD-4's. No BD-4C appears in it.

From the NTSB avdata databases (pre-1982, pre-2008 and avall, downloaded 2026-10-29; R): **62 US events, 17 fatal, 27 killed aboard**, 1971 to 2026. A 1972 midair killed two people in the other aircraft. Aviation Safety Network lists 56 BD-4 accidents worldwide (B). No exposure data was found, so no rate. Against about 308 ever-registered aircraft it is a heavier record than the Cozy MKIV's (22 events, 7 fatal, on about 350 built), over a fleet that has flown three decades longer.

What recurs, by keyword over the coded causes and narratives (our count; a guide, not a classification):

- **Stall or spin:** 12 events, 6 of them fatal. The record says pilot
  airspeed control, not the airframe. There is no stabilator- or
  wing-related cause in the record.
- **Fuel:** 14 events, 2 fatal. Two are coded to design: a 1990 "FAILURE OF
  THE MANUFACTURER (AMATEUR BUILT) TO DESIGN FUEL SYSTEM ADEQUATELY TO PREVENT
  VAPOR LOCK", and a 1971 starvation coded poor design. The builders' FAQ
  says starvation happened "Mostly ... in high angle of attack situations
  shortly after take-off", because "The wings have no dihedral". The BD-4C
  wing adds a fibreglass sump and pickup fins for this (F, P). They have not
  been tested in service at any scale.
- **Propeller blade separations:** three, one fatal (2014, CEN14LA305).
- **One in-flight airframe failure:** an aileron and flap separated in 1971
  (N4381F). It was not fatal. It contradicts the builders' site's claim of no
  catastrophic in-flight structural failure, though not its narrower FAQ
  claim of no wing-spar failure.

**A structural flag for whoever owns structures, not a finding.** The BD-4C sheet states ultimate load factors "BD-4C @ 2000 Lb. gross: Positive 6.3 G", and claims that "The BD-4C, as a 4-place 2400-Lb. gross airplane, currently exceeds the required standards for the 'Normal' FAA certification category". If strength is fixed, 6.3 g at 2,000 lb is 5.25 g at 2,400 lb. That is under the 5.7 g ultimate (3.8 x 1.5) of the normal category. The 2013 BD-4B sheet made the same normal-category claim at 2,000 lb gross, not 2,400. The BD-4C has a new wing and spar, which may account for the difference; the published figures do not show it. The builders' FAQ says long-wing BD-4s on the original spars should stay at or under 2,200 lb. Nothing here clears or condemns the structure.

## Reference check (AER-4145)

Our own check, from published geometry and weights. Method, calibration and the Aeromomentum assumptions are in [docs/SHORTLIST.md](../SHORTLIST.md). The BD-4C-specific study is `docs/shortlist/check/bede/bede_check.py`, with its output in `bede_check_output.md` beside it. Nothing was flown or weighed by us, and nobody has been contacted.

### Verdict

**Reference: a four-seater with real useful load and a short range, faster
on paper than in the one airplane measured.**

- **Useful load.** Bede's 1,150 lb is credible. One built BD-4C weighs 31 lb
  over the sheet. Fleet weights of the older BD-4 run 50-90 lb over it, with
  a builders' range up to 1,500 lb.
- **Speed.** Bede's speeds need a drag factor of 1.15-1.20 at 8,000 ft. That
  is at the clean end of the measured fleet (1.16-1.36), so plausible but
  optimistic. The only measured BD-4C, flown before its fairings were fitted,
  needed about 1.77.
- **Range.** The published range fits 51 gal at economy power with no
  reserve. Only the 80 gal option comes near 1,000 nm.
- **On the AM20T:** 180-192 KTAS, 1,087 lb useful, 572-590 nm on 51 gal or
  927-971 nm on 80 gal, all on factory empty weight and the fleet drag factor.

### Our numbers against Bede's

From `bede/bede_check_output.md` (E). Bede states no altitude, so the drag factor that matches each published speed is given at 8,000 ft, the highest altitude at which these normally aspirated engines still make 75%. At any lower altitude a larger factor is needed.

| Bede's figure | Our KTAS at the same power, fleet factor 1.29: sea level / 8,000 ft | Factor to match at 8,000 ft |
|---|---|---|
| 183 mph (159 KTAS), 75% of 180 hp | 146 / 155 | 1.20 |
| 174 mph (151 KTAS), 65% of 180 hp | 137 / 145 | 1.15 |
| 192 mph (167 KTAS), 75% of 200 hp | 152 / 162 | 1.19 |
| 183 mph (159 KTAS), 65% of 200 hp | 143 / 152 | 1.15 |

- **Speed.** Plausible at altitude, if optimistic: 1.15-1.20 sits at the
  clean end of the measured band (Express 1.16, P210N 1.29, Cozy 1.36). The
  model's own uncertainties are about the same size:
  - the slab-sided fuselage, whose box perimeter is 1.27 times the model's
    ellipse: -4.5 kt;
  - a laminar run of 0.10 instead of 0.25 on the bonded wing: -3.1 kt;
  - gear drag 0.45-0.80 ft2 instead of 0.60: +4.2 to -5.0 kt.

  With the box fuselage, Bede's speeds would need about 1.05-1.10, cleaner
  than any measured airplane in the survey.
- **The one measured BD-4C reads far draggier.** N2468Z's 2019 points need a
  factor of 1.76-1.78, even with an unfaired-gear allowance (D/q 0.90 ft2).
  But the airplane was unfinished: the builder lists no gear fairings,
  unsealed wing-root and tip gaps, and long exhaust stacks. His later
  "142 knots TAS" at 65%, still unfaired, falls between our 4,000 and 8,000 ft
  figures at the fleet factor. **No measurement of a finished, faired BD-4C
  exists.**
- **Range.** The published 892 nm (180 hp) needs 48-62 gal with no reserve
  at 55-75% power, and the 782 nm (200 hp) needs 45-58 gal (BSFC 0.40-0.45).
  So it is 51 gal, no reserve, at economy power, lean. On 80 gal the same
  settings give 1,086-1,528 nm. The builder's 717 nm on 48 gal at 65%
  matches our 717 nm with unfaired gear at BSFC 0.40.

### Construction with modern home tools (the axis Bill asked about)

**Score: 4 of 5** (AERO's judgement). The scale, against MANUFACTURING's reference home shop:
- 5: hand tools and that shop, no welding, no molds and no controlled process;
- 4: one controlled process to learn, or factory-made critical parts;
- 3: molds or welding;
- 2: a service for primary structure;
- 1: not a home build.

Part by part:
- **Fuselage: 5.** A bolted truss of pre-formed angles and precut gussets.
  Bede: "No welding, no complicated jigs". The truss sets its own geometry, so
  the builder needs a drill, rivet tools and patience, not a jig table. The
  flat .016 and .020 in skins come precut in the kit.
- **Wing: 4.** The critical parts come from the factory: CNC-cut honeycomb
  ribs and the tube spar. The builder still bonds the skins and seals the
  tank bays. Structural bonding of aluminium is a process (surface
  preparation, adhesive, cure, inspection), and its quality is not visible
  after the fact. Bede offers a three-day Build Center session for the wing,
  which says where builders need help.
- **Tail: 5 for the builder.** It arrives bonded and assembled.

The hours are the strongest evidence: about 700 h (F). Against the survey,
the metal Aries runs 2,500-3,500 h as a kit and 3,100-4,700 h home-cut, and
the Cozy about 2,500 h (MANUFACTURING, stage 4). One BD-4C builder took seven
years of spare time (B), so 700 h is a factory figure.

The weight evidence is thin but good. The one built BD-4C on record is 31 lb
over the sheet; the Cozy CAFE aircraft was 129 lb over its designer figure.
For comparison on the same scale (AERO's judgement): the Cozy is a 3 (no
molds, but 2,500 h of foam, glass and fill); the Aries kit is a 4 (matched
holes and riveting), and the Aries home-cut route a 3.

### The deep-dive axes: storage, home-shop construction, safety, ride

**Aspect ratio 6.6**, constant chord.

- **Storage.** 26.2 ft span: fits a 40 ft T-hangar door, the shortest span in
  the survey. The wings come off: each panel sleeves over the centre-section
  spar with a 12 in overlap held by bolts (P, Kitplanes 2018), and the flaps
  and ailerons "quickly disconnect from the fuselage when removing the wing"
  (F). Bede lists a 7 ft folded width (F), but the folding option is rarely
  bought: "we haven't sold one in years" (P, Kitplanes 2018). We found no
  builder account of how long removal takes.
- **Construction.** Above.
- **Safety.**
  - Best L/D about 12.3 at 107 KTAS (prop drag excluded), the lowest of the
    conventional wings in the survey (14-17).
  - Stall 59 kt with flaps at gross (F).
  - On the AM20T at continuous power the BD-4C cruises over Bede's 146 kt
    maximum rough-air speed: about 160 KIAS at 8,000 ft and about 150 KIAS at
    16,000 ft (our TAS, standard day). Bede states that limit at 2,000 lb. On
    this engine the airplane throttles back in turbulence, or needs its
    rough-air speed re-derived.
- **Ride.** Gust dn 1.49 at 23.1 lb/ft2: about the Cozy's (1.51).

### Independent performance check

Model: `docs/shortlist/check/models/bede-bd-4c.json` (51 gal) and `bede-bd-4c-80gal.json`; results in `docs/shortlist/check/results/`. Method in [SHORTLIST.md](../SHORTLIST.md#method).

**Wetted areas (OpenVSP CompGeom, parametric model from published dimensions):** Fuselage 207 ft2, Wing 204 ft2, HTail 41 ft2, Fin 17 ft2; total 469 ft2.

**Model assumptions** (grade E unless a source is named):

- Wing span 26.2 ft (F); area 103.9 ft2 from Bede's 23.10 lb/ft2 at 2,400 lb (F, derived); constant chord 3.97 ft (E); NACA 64-415 modified, t/c 0.15 (F).
- Fuselage 22.6 ft (F); width 4.2 ft from the 46 in cabin, height 4.5 ft (E). Modelled elliptic, though the real fuselage is slab-sided; the sensitivity is above.
- Stabilator 7.5 x 3.0 ft, NACA 63-009 (F). Fin 9 ft2 (E, vertical-tail volume 0.04).
- Laminar run: wing 0.25 (bonded, rivet-free skin; the same credit as the Aries), tails 0.15, fuselage 0.05.
- Fixed tricycle gear with spats, D/q 0.60 ft2, the Express's allowance (E).

### Engine fit with the Aeromomentum line

- **Power.** Bede recommends 180-210 hp and publishes 180, 200 and 260 hp
  columns. The AM20T's assumed 195 hp continuous replaces the 200 hp IO-360
  almost exactly. Its 260 hp rating matches Bede's 260 hp column, which Bede
  gives at 1,450 lb empty and 950 lb useful (F).
- **Mass and balance.** The installation adds about 63 lb at the nose of a
  tractor (E). That moves the CG forward, the benign direction, where on the
  Cozy it moved aft.
- **Precedent.** SureWings' BD-4C-S package specifies a 195 hp Viking (P), an
  automotive-derived liquid-cooled engine. A liquid-cooled installation in
  this nose is therefore already being done.
- **Fuel.** At 195 hp the 51 gal minimum gives 572-590 nm. The 80 gal option
  is the configuration to compare with the mission.
- **Speed limits.** Above, under safety: continuous power at either assumed
  critical altitude exceeds the published rough-air speed.
