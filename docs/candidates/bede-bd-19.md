# Bede BD-19 (reference, projected)

Reference design, added 2026-10-29 at Bill's request (AER-4145), alongside the
[Bede BD-4C](bede-bd-4c.md). It is a new kit, announced in 2026. It is not a
revival candidate and is not ranked. **No BD-19 has flown, and every number
Bede publishes for it is a projection, copied from the BD-4C's spec sheet.**
Read this dossier with the BD-4C's, which holds the shared wing, gear, check
and sources. Nobody has been contacted.

## Lineage: what the BD-19 takes from the BD-4C and the BD-18

- **The wing is the BD-4C's.** "Bede's 10' wing" is each outer panel on its
  10 ft tubular spar. Builders sell a "Standard spar set (10 ft)" for the BD-4
  (B, [BD-4 classifieds](http://old.bd-4.org/classifieds_old.php)). Bede calls
  it "the same proven wing that we use on our BD-4C". It is a bonded wet wing
  with honeycomb ribs, a NACA 64-415, 51-80 gal on the BD-4C (see its
  dossier). On the BD-19 it moves from the top of the fuselage to the bottom.
  That changes the fuel feed: a low wing feeds by gravity or by pump, which
  the no-dihedral high wing's fuel history (BD-4C dossier) makes worth asking
  about. It also changes the wing-body junction drag and how the wing comes
  off. Bede publishes nothing on any of the three.
- **The gear is the BD-4C's.** S-ply spring mains, castering nose wheel (F).
- **"It will share a lot of the certified components from the BD-18" (F).**
  What that can mean, from the record:
  - **There have been two BD-18s.** The first, announced in 2003, was a
    two-seat derivative of the single-seat BD-17. It had a 25 ft 6 in span, a
    47 in cabin, 1,250-1,335 lb gross and 43 gal (F, [jimbede.com, Wayback
    2011](https://web.archive.org/web/20110519051155/http://www.jimbede.com/airplane_designs/BD-18/Pages/BD18-Specifications.html)).
    By 2014 Bedecorp listed it "Not Currently In Production" (F, Wayback).
  - **The 2026 BD-18** ([jimbede.com/bd-18](https://jimbede.com/bd-18/),
    [Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20261029131316/https://jimbede.com/bd-18/))
    is larger: two seats, low wing, 32 ft span, 2,000 lb MTOW, 1,250 lb empty,
    48 gal, a Continental IO-370-CL, $290,000. Bede says it "will be
    available both SLSA and ELSA", and "We are about 35% through the
    certification process" (F). Phase 1 flight test was "scheduled to be
    completed late October". No first flight, N-number or photograph of a
    flying BD-18 is published, and no Bede BD-18 is in the FAA registry.
  - **A special light-sport aircraft is not type-certificated.** Under
    14 CFR 21.190 it is issued an airworthiness certificate on the maker's
    statement of compliance with an FAA-accepted consensus standard.
  - **So "certified components" can mean, at most, type-certificated parts
    bought in.** The IO-370-CL engine holds a type certificate (Wikipedia
    gives 2018-10-11, not checked against the TCDS); so may the propeller and
    avionics. It cannot mean certified BD-18 structure: none exists yet. The
    BD-19's 47 in cabin width is the same figure Bede gives for both BD-18s.
    That suggests a shared cross-section, but it is our inference, not Bede's
    statement.

## Screen (published numbers; like for like)

- **As published (projected):** the same as the BD-4C's sheet.
  - Useful load: 1,150 lb, 15% over the target.
  - Range: 782-892 nm, 11-22% short, basis not stated.
  - Cruise: 159 KTAS at 75% on 180 hp, 1 kt under the floor; 167 KTAS on
    200 hp, over it. No altitude is stated.
  - Payload at 1,000 nm: none on the published range.
- **Measured or reported:** none. Nothing has flown.
- **Aeromomentum fit (first cut):** yes, at 180-200 hp, as the BD-4C.

## Why it died

Not dead, and not yet born: the first kit packages are to ship 2026-11-01.

## Idea ledger (what a new airplane should keep; AERO's judgement)

- **A parts count as a design target.** Bede publishes "less than 400 parts"
  for a four-seater. That is a number a home-shop design can be held to. The
  BD-19 has not yet shown it in a shipped kit.
- **Re-using a proven wing on a new fuselage.** The wing, spar and gear carry
  over from a flying type, so the new structure is the fuselage. That is the
  cheapest way to a new four-seat kit. It still needs its own flight test:
  moving the wing from high to low changes stall behaviour, fuel feed and
  balance.

## Rights and provenance (known so far; research, not legal advice)

Bede Aero LLC (Ohio, entity 4644785) offers the BD-19 on jimbede.com. The BD-4C licence or sale to Gratia Aero in 2024 (BD-4C dossier) is described as covering "the BD-4C airframe" (P, Aero-News). Whether it reaches the BD-4C wing as used on the BD-19 is not public. No trademark or patent was found. It is a reference, so there is no buy-or-re-derive question.

## Safety record

None: it has not flown. The BD-4 record (BD-4C dossier) belongs to a high-wing airplane with a different fuel system, and transfers only to the shared wing and gear.

## Reference check (AER-4145)

### Verdict

**Reference, projected: there is no BD-19 to check.**

- **No airplane and no geometry.** Bede publishes no BD-19 span, length,
  wing area, fuel or engine, and its performance table is the BD-4C's.
- **So our check is the BD-4C's, used as a stand-in.** On the AM20T that
  gives 180-192 KTAS, 1,087 lb useful, and 572-590 nm on 51 gal or
  927-971 nm on 80 gal (see the BD-4C dossier).
- **What the low wing changes is not modelled.** It has a different frontal
  area under a canopy, a different wing-body junction and ground effect.
  Without a BD-19 geometry we cannot say whether it ends up cleaner or
  draggier than the BD-4C.
- **A real BD-19 check needs Bede's three-view or measurements of the first
  airplane.** Until then the BD-19 is the BD-4C's numbers with a wider cabin
  and a "less than 400 parts" claim.

### The deep-dive axes

- **Storage.** The BD-4C wing comes off (BD-4C dossier). Span not published.
- **Construction with modern home tools: 4 of 5 projected** (the scale is in
  the BD-4C dossier). The score rests on the same wing and the same
  angle-and-sheet construction, and on "less than 400 parts". No kit has
  shipped and no build time is published. The score could rise if the new
  fuselage really needs no jig, or fall if the canopy and turtle deck bring
  molded or formed parts. "Canopy and Turtle Deck Components" is a $10,900
  package (F), the third-largest of seven.
- **Safety.** Unflown. Stall speeds are the BD-4C's, copied.
- **Ride.** The BD-4C wing at the same gross gives the BD-4C's gust response
  (dn 1.49).

### Engine fit with the Aeromomentum line

As the BD-4C: 180-200 hp published, so the AM20T at an assumed 195 hp continuous fits. The BD-4C's speed-limit flag applies with it: on the AM20T both cruise cases exceed the 146 kt maximum rough-air speed on Bede's BD-4C sheet. The BD-19 page gives only Vne.
