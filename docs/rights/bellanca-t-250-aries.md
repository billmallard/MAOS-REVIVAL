# Bellanca T-250 Aries (Anderson Greenwood Model 51) — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon. **Board issue:**
AER-3805. **Date:** 2026-10-24. **Status:** first pass, desk research from
public sources only. Nobody has been contacted. Added at Bill's request in his
review (AER-3783); AERO is adding it to the long list.

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **The TC is live, and it was never Bellanca's.** TCDS A11SW covers Models 51
  and 51A. It is "Current", Rev 2 of 1 July 1980, and the holder is "Anderson,
  Greenwood and Company, Houston, Texas", with "(Bellanca)" only in the heading
  (FAA DRS, P). "T-250 Aries" is the marketing name. The FAA model is "51".
- **Bellanca only ever held a licence, and a court valued it at zero.** The
  Eighth Circuit, in Bellanca's bankruptcy, described "Bellanca's exclusive
  right, granted by Aviation [Anderson-Greenwood Aviation Corp.], to manufacture
  and market the aircraft named 'Aries T-250'". Because Aviation's consent to
  assign "could be withheld for any reason whatsoever", the licence had no market
  value. The bankruptcy court called the right to assign "the functional
  equivalent of no right whatsoever" (850 F.2d 1275, 8th Cir. 1988, P). So the
  type design stayed with the Anderson Greenwood side when Bellanca failed in
  1980.
- **The holder of record has since been absorbed at least twice.** Keystone
  International acquired Anderson, Greenwood & Co. in 1986, and Tyco Flow Control
  acquired Keystone in 1997 (company history; Wikipedia; L). "Anderson
  Greenwood" is now a valve brand listed by Emerson (emerson.com, P). The steps
  between Tyco and Emerson were not verified here. **Whether the A11SW
  type certificate, and the separate Aviation subsidiary, passed down that chain
  or were kept, sold or dissolved along the way is not public.** DRS has not
  changed the holder line since 1980.
- **Fleet: 4 on the register, 6 serials known.** Model 51 records: N251AG (s/n
  0001, 1976, the prototype, registered experimental), N250DJ (0003-80), N77CT
  (0004-80) and N250MG (0005-80), all standard normal. Two more, 0006-80 and
  0007-80, were deregistered in 2010-2011 (FAA registry, P). Plane & Pilot
  reports four customer aircraft plus a structural test airframe (S).
- **It had a production certificate.** "S/N 3 and up Bellanca Aircraft
  Corporation, Alexandria, Minnesota; Production Certificate No. 1GL" (TCDS, P).
  The PC was Bellanca's and lapsed with Bellanca. The TC was not Bellanca's.

## Holder or seller today

- **Anderson, Greenwood & Co.** is the holder of record. The 8th Circuit opinion
  names two Texas corporations: Anderson, Greenwood & Co. (AGCO) and its
  subsidiary Anderson-Greenwood Aviation Corp. (Aviation), which granted
  Bellanca the licence (P). The Texas Comptroller's active franchise-tax list
  has no entity with "ANDERSON GREENWOOD" in its name (P). The same dataset does
  return the known Lancair entities, so the query works.
- **Corporate succession.** "In 1986, Keystone acquired Anderson, Greenwood"
  (Funding Universe company history, L). Keystone was "acquired by Tyco Flow
  Control in 1997, with Anderson Greenwood being maintained as a subsidiary"
  (Wikipedia, "Anderson Greenwood Crosby", L). Emerson now lists Anderson
  Greenwood among its valve brands (emerson.com, P). How it got from Tyco to
  Emerson was not verified here.
- **Reading.** If the TC followed the valve business, today's holder is inside
  Emerson. That is a large company with no aircraft line, which may regard A11SW
  as worthless, as the bankruptcy court did the licence. If it did not follow,
  the TC may be orphaned. Either way, the first step is the same: find out who
  actually holds A11SW.

## Drawings, molds and tooling

- **Who had them.** Bellanca built the production aircraft in Alexandria under
  its own PC (TCDS, P), so production tooling was in Minnesota in 1980. The
  design data belonged to AGCO / Aviation (8th Cir., P).
- **Where they are now:** unknown. The 8th Circuit opinion deals with aircraft
  transfers and the licence's value, not tooling. The bankruptcy court's
  63-page opinion (56 B.R. 339, Bankr. D. Minn. 1985) was not read; it is the
  next source to check.
- **Construction:** all-metal, with a laminar-flow wing and a fuselage with an
  intentional "near absence of curved lines aft the engine" for easy production
  (Wikipedia, citing AOPA Pilot, March 1977, L). Airfoil: NACA "60-412" (UIUC
  airfoil list, S).

## Why it died

Anderson Greenwood bought control of Bellanca to build the Aries. Bellanca was
weaker than expected, having just spent heavily on the composite Skyrocket II,
and went into Chapter 11 in July 1980 (8th Cir., P; Plane & Pilot, S). The Aries
production run ended with it. The design itself was not the cause on the record.
Its owner was a valve company, and the 1976 arrangement licensed manufacture
to Bellanca rather than building the aircraft itself (8th Cir., P). The 1985
bankruptcy opinion has the full account of why; it was not read.

## Where an aviation IP attorney is needed

1. **Who holds A11SW today**, and whether it passed with the Anderson Greenwood
   valve business through Keystone, Tyco and on to Emerson, or stayed with a
   shell that was later dissolved.
2. **Anderson-Greenwood Aviation Corp.**: whether it still exists, or what
   happened to its assets, since it, not AGCO, granted the licence.
3. **The 1985 bankruptcy record** (56 B.R. 339): whether the estate or its
   buyers took any claim to tooling or data.
