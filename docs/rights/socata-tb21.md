# Socata TB-21 Trinidad TC — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon (census legwork
delegated, judgement mine). **Board issue:** AER-3787. **Date:** 2026-10-24.
**Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only. Nobody has been
contacted. From AERO's stage 1 long list (near miss: useful load -20 lb).

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **The TC is live and well held.** EASA TCDS EASA.A.378 (TB 10, TB 9, TB 20,
  TB 21, TB 200), Issue 06 of 24 April 2026, names **Daher Aircraft SAS, Orly**.
  FAA TCDS A51EU Rev 18 (30 August 2021) is "Current" and names Daher Aerospace
  (P).
- **The holder is large, solvent and still supports the type.** Daher builds the
  TBM and Kodiak. Owner press says the TB line is still supported (S).
- **The TB-21 shares one TC with four sister models.** A sale or licence would
  cover a family TC that supports about 2,000 aircraft, not just the TB-21.
  That makes Daher less likely to let it go.
- **No sale or licence of TB rights was found.** Searches for 2024-2026 news
  turned up support and ADs only (S). Daher dropped the TB as an order option by
  2012 (L).
- **US fleet:** 50 TB21 on the FAA registry, plus 7 under an odd "TB21TC" code (P).
- **"SOCATA" is a live US mark** (EADS SOCATA, class 12). No live "TRINIDAD"
  aircraft mark was found (P).

## Holder or seller today

- **Daher** is a private French industrial group. Under its "Take Off 2027"
  plan, the TBM and Kodiak moved into a new Daher Aircraft division from 1
  January 2026 (AIN, 7 Feb 2024; Daher press release; S/P). The EASA rename
  matches this (P).
- **Support:** AOPA (2017) says "Socata still provides product support"; Aviation
  Consumer (2011) says SOCATA Aircraft USA "still supports all models" (S). An
  earlier version said Daher ships airframe parts from France and the US; neither
  source says that, and it is withdrawn.
  Daher has opened a new spares and support centre in western France that covers
  legacy GA types (AviTrader; S). A New Zealand AD schedule of 31 July 2025 still
  lists the TB family as Daher Aerospace products (P).
- **No evidence that Daher has sold, licensed or offered TB rights.** None was
  found in the press or in Daher's public statements.

## Drawings, molds and tooling

- The TB-21 type design (drawing list ED 184/85 and related) is referenced on the
  live EASA TCDS. That implies Daher holds it as TC holder (P).
- **Tooling: unknown.** SOCATA planned to move TB20/TB21 final assembly to
  Aerostar, Bacau (Romania), in 2007 (L, Wikipedia citing Flight). Whether that
  happened, and where the jigs are now, was not found.

## Kit or plans rights

None. Certified type.

## Patents and trademarks

- **Patents.** No TB-specific patents found. 1980s patents expired long ago.
- **"SOCATA"**, SN 77702413, class 12 airplanes, owner EADS SOCATA: **live**
  (USPTO trademark search, P). "DAHER-SOCATA" (SN 85483470) is cancelled.
- **"DAHER"**, SN 79033240 and 79179240: live, but the class 12 goods are cancelled
  (P).
- **"TRINIDAD":** no live class 12 aircraft mark found. The only class 12 hit is
  a cancelled boat mark (P). "TB 320" and "TANGARA TB 320" (SOCATA) are abandoned.
- **Positive control:** the same search returns the live Textron "MUSTANG"
  aircraft mark, SN 76436781 (P).
- A revival should not use SOCATA or Daher names. "Trinidad" looks clear in the
  US register but was not cleared outside the US.

## Fleet

- FAA registry, make SOCATA, model TB21, 2026-10-24: **50 aircraft**, code
  8680697, type certificated (P). Another **7** sit under code 8680699 "TB21TC",
  recorded as "Amateur" and "Turbo-prop". That looks like a registry coding error
  (P, coding as published).
- **Positive control:** the same query for MOONEY M22 returns 14 (P).
- **Built:** TB-21 count not found. The whole TB family was about 2,150 by 2007
  (L).

## Why it died

Sales fell after 2001. SOCATA suspended serial production in 2003 and tried to
move work to cheaper sites. From 2008 the TB GT was build-to-order only, and by
2012 it had gone as an option (Wikipedia, citing Flight International; L/S).
Daher put its money into the TBM turboprop.

## The paths

| Path | What it would take | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Buy the TC** | Approach Daher Aircraft SAS for A.378 / A51EU. | Unlikely. The TC covers five models and about 2,000 aircraft that Daher still supports and earns parts revenue from. Requires contact; not done. |
| **License** | A licence for the TB-21 type design only, or a production licence. | More realistic than a sale. China weighed licensed TB-20 production in 1988 (L), so there is a precedent of interest. Requires contact. |
| **Modify existing airframes** | STC for an Aeromomentum install on existing TB-21s. 50+ US airframes, many more in Europe. | The most practical path. It needs no rights from Daher for the airframe, only STC work. Parts supply still depends on Daher. |
| **Re-derive from an airframe** | Measure a TB-21 and build a new design. | Physically easy (metal airframe, many examples). **Attorney needed** on design rights and trade dress. |

## Where an aviation IP attorney is needed

1. **Scope of any licence** that would cover only the TB-21 out of a five-model TC.
2. **Re-derivation of a metal airframe** from a live, supported TC whose holder
   is active: copyright in drawings, French design rights, unfair competition.
3. **Name clearance** for "Trinidad" outside the US, and the live SOCATA mark.
