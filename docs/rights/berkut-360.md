# Berkut 360 — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon (census legwork
delegated, judgement mine). **Board issue:** AER-3787. **Date:** 2026-10-24.
**Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only. Nobody has been
contacted. From AERO's stage 1 long list (near miss: useful load 965 lb, 35 lb
short; speed unconditioned).

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **The rights have changed hands at least three times, and the last known
  holder died in 2009.** The chain runs from the Ronneberg-Murphy partnership
  (1989-92), to Experimental Aviation, Inc. (bankrupt), to Renaissance
  Composites (1996), to Vicki Cruse's Berkut Engineering and Design, Inc. That
  company withdrew the kit in 2002 (Wikipedia, L). **When and how the assets
  reached Cruse is not settled:** the "January 2001" sale from Renaissance came
  from a Wikipedia sentence tagged "failed verification" in 2011 and removed in
  June 2026, and Kitplanes says Cruse "formed Berkut Engineering in the early
  1990s" (S). Corrected 2026-10-24.
- **Cruse kept ownership until her death on 22 August 2009** (date: Wikipedia,
  L; Kitplanes reported the death on 24 August 2009, S). Who holds the molds, drawings and kit rights since then is
  **not shown by any public record found.** This is the decisive open fact.
- **The designer is still active.** Dave Ronneberg is the registered owner of
  two aircraft built by "Berkut Engineering and Design" (model Mobius), one
  dated 2009, at Santa Monica. He co-owns a Berkut registered in 2018 (FAA
  registry, P). He may hold or control the tooling. Not confirmed.
- **The company built real molds.** By 2002 the kit had "over 100 molded
  components", including molded wings and canard (Berkut Engineering website,
  printed 30 May 2002, in an NTSB docket; S).
- **The design is a reworked Long-EZ.** Ronneberg bought the second set of
  Long-EZ plans ever sold and evolved it into the Berkut (same source, S). No
  RAF licence for the Berkut as a kit was found.
- **No BERKUT aircraft trademark exists** (USPTO, P).
- **Fleet:** 24 registered (FAA registry, P). About 75 kits sold and 20
  completed (Wikipedia, L).

## Holder or seller today

- **No seller.** The kit was withdrawn in 2002 and no successor seller was
  found.
- **Ownership chain** (Wikipedia, L, unless noted):
  - 1989-1992: Ronneberg and Donald S. Murphy, as the partnership Experimental
    Aviation. Murphy wanted to shelve the project; Ronneberg wanted kits.
  - 1992: Ronneberg incorporates Experimental Aviation, Inc. and sells kits.
    Deliveries began January 1993 (Berkut site 2002, S).
  - Lawsuits between the two bankrupted Ronneberg, Murphy and the company.
  - 1996: Richard Riley and Renaissance Composites revive the kit, with
    Ronneberg as consultant.
  - Assets reach Vicki Cruse's Berkut Engineering and Design, Inc.: January
    2001 per a since-removed, failed-verification Wikipedia sentence (L), or a
    company formed "in the early 1990s" per Kitplanes (S). Unresolved. The 2002
    website names Ronneberg and Cruse as principals (S).
  - 2002: kit withdrawn. A 2003 deal with Republic Aerospace fell through.
    Ronneberg turned the project toward UAVs.
  - 22 August 2009: Cruse dies at the World Aerobatic Championships in England.
- **Corporate status of Berkut Engineering and Design, Inc. and Experimental
  Aviation, Inc.: not verified.** The California Secretary of State's site
  blocked automated access.
- **Support:** none from a company. Builders support each other.

## Drawings, molds and tooling

- **Tooling existed and was extensive.** By 2002: over 100 molded parts, molded
  canard and wings with winglets, and in-house landing gear (Berkut site,
  printed 30 May 2002, S).
- **Landing gear rights.** Ronneberg "acquired the rights to use" Shirl Dickey's
  E-Racer retractable gear and later built it himself (same, S). Any gear
  revival would also need that cleared.
- **Where the molds are now: unknown.** Candidates are Cruse's estate,
  Ronneberg, or a buyer of the company. The Berkut 540 registered to "Nordic
  Composites/Renaissance Com" and Renaissance's four-place "8-F" show
  Renaissance also built related airframes (FAA registry, P).
- **Prototype:** Ronneberg's aircraft. "RONNEBERG DAVE | RONNEBERG BERKUT"
  (N95MS, 1999) is now owned by another person (FAA registry, P). Which
  airframe is the 1991 prototype was not established.

## Kit or plans rights

- Kit rights passed with the company assets in 1996 (Wikipedia, L) and later to
  Cruse's company, date unresolved (above). No later transfer is public.
- **Rutan lineage.** Ronneberg held an RAF Long-EZ builder licence (bought the
  second plans set; S). RAF licensed one airplane per plans set and stopped
  licensing in 1985 (see the Defiant file). Whether a kit derived from a
  Long-EZ needed RAF consent was never tested in public.

## Patents and trademarks

- **Patents.** None identified. Any patent filed 1989-2002 has expired.
- **"BERKUT":** one USPTO record in total, a cancelled vodka mark (Class 33).
  No aircraft mark, live or dead (USPTO search index, P).
- Positive control: the same search for "MOONEY" returns live SN 72336652,
  aircraft and parts (P).

## Fleet

- FAA registry, ReleasableAircraft database downloaded 2026-10-24 (P):
  **24** registrations with "BERKUT" in the make or model (360, 540, FG, HBX),
  after removing two Mobius aircraft built by Berkut Engineering. Builder-entered
  names, so the count is approximate. Positive control: the same method returns
  14 for the Mooney M22.
- **Built:** about 75 kits sold and 20 completed; 31 registered across all
  variants at some point (Wikipedia, L).

## Why it died

Founder-partner lawsuits bankrupted the first company. Two later owners kept the
kit going, but Berkut Engineering withdrew it in 2002 and the designer moved to
UAV work (Wikipedia, L). The owner's death in 2009 left the assets' fate
unclear.
