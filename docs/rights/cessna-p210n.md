# Cessna P210N Pressurized Centurion — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon. **Board issue:** AER-3787.
**Date:** 2026-10-24. **Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only.
Nobody has been contacted. Fed by AERO, stage 1 batch 1.

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

## Summary

- **A live type held by a large OEM.** The TC is held by Textron Aviation Inc.
  (Cessna's successor), is not orphaned, and Textron still supports the fleet.
- **The engineering data is Textron's and is not for sale on any public record.**
- **The fleet is large:** 418 piston P210Ns on the US registry, plus 3 turboprop
  conversions. That makes the "improve existing airframes" path real.
- **The practical path is modification, not revival:** buy used airframes and
  improve them under supplemental type certificates (STCs). This path is already
  proven: the Silver Eagle turbine conversion exists, with its STCs now held by
  Griggs Aircraft Refinishing.
- **A new-build copy needs counsel.** Building new P210s, certified or
  experimental, from data that is not ours is an attorney question.

## Fleet

- FAA registry, make/model `CESSNA P210N`, queried 2026-10-24: **418** piston
  aircraft, plus **3** registered as turboprop (the Silver Eagle conversions under
  the same model code). This matches AERO's figure. Production: 834 (AERO).

## Drawings and tooling

- Held by Textron Aviation as proprietary type design data. There is no public
  record of the P210 data being sold, licensed or released.
- Textron states it supports legacy piston types through its own engineering and
  supply chain (it bought Able Aerospace in 2016 for repair and overhaul work).
  The P210 is out of production; Textron no longer builds complex piston singles,
  and in November 2025 it announced it would also end Bonanza and Baron production.

## Kit or plans rights

None. It is a certified production type; no kit or plans rights exist.

## Patents and trademarks

- Every patent from the P210N's design era (production 1978-86) has expired.
- "Cessna" and "Centurion" are Textron marks. A USPTO check was not run; it is not
  needed to decide anything, because a revival would not use these names in any case.

## STCs on this airframe

- **Silver Eagle** turbine conversion: Rolls-Royce (Allison) 250-B17F/2, derated
  to 450 hp, about 215 KTAS at altitude. O&N Aircraft developed it from about 1992.
  O&N closed in 2016 after Myron Olson's death, and Griggs Aircraft Refinishing took
  over the STCs. A conversion cost about $1 million (AOPA, 2018; Aviation Consumer).

This is the template for the modification path. It is also a warning on cost.

## The paths

| Path | What it would take | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Buy the TC** | A transaction with Textron. | No public sign that Textron sells legacy TCs. It would need contact; not done. |
| **License** | A licence from Textron for the type design data. | Same. |
| **Modify existing airframes** | Buy used P210Ns and certify improvements by STC (engine, wing, systems), with Textron data where we can get it and our own substantiation where we cannot. | Proven route, with a fleet of 418. This is the real path for a certified P210, but it improves airplanes; it does not build new ones. |
| **Re-derive from an airframe** | Measure an airframe and build a new design from it, certified or experimental. | Physically possible: airframes are plentiful. Legally it is the hardest question in this batch. **Attorney needed.** |

## Where an aviation IP attorney is needed

1. **Re-deriving from a measured airframe.** What Textron's rights in the type
   design data reach, and whether a new design measured from a P210N, certified or
   experimental, would infringe them.
2. **Using Textron data under an STC.** The terms of any data access, if we
   modify airframes.
