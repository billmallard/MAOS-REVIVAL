# Mooney 301 — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon (census legwork
delegated, judgement mine). **Board issue:** AER-3787. **Date:** 2026-10-24.
**Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only. Nobody has been
contacted. From AERO's stage 1 long list (pass). **Updated 2026-10-24** for
AERO's stage 3 question (rank 3): does the design sit with Mooney or with Daher,
and does any type certificate, drawing or patent survive either path? See
"Mooney or Daher" below.

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **There is no type certificate.** No FAA TCDS exists for the Mooney 301 or
  M30. The absence check was run with a positive control (FAA DRS, P).
- **The registry knew it as a Mooney M30, N301MX, s/n 0000.** That registration
  was cancelled on 26 July 1989. The N-number now belongs to an unrelated Aviat
  A-1 (FAA registry, P).
- **The only airframe was scrapped.** Its wings were cut off and the rest went
  to an A&P school in Abilene, Texas, after the Mooney/Socata lawsuits settled
  (Mooney Events, citing Mooney staff, L/S).
- **All M30 engineering data went to Socata in Tarbes in 1987**, with the
  dismantled prototype (Mooney Events, L/S; Plane & Pilot, S). Who owns that data
  now, Mooney International or Daher, is not shown by any public record found.
- **The TBM 700 is a separate design.** Its TC (FAA A60EU, EASA.A.010) is held by
  Daher Aircraft SAS and does not mention Mooney (P). Socata used its own
  fuselage and wing shapes and kept the 301's flight-control layout (L/S).
- **Two companies may claim the 301:** Mooney International (successor to the
  developer) and Daher (successor to the joint venture partner that took the
  data). The joint venture terms and the lawsuit settlement are not public.
- **Answer to stage 3: unresolved on the public record, and nothing survives
  either path that a revival could use.** No TC on either side for the 301, no
  patent (and any from the era expired by about 2012), no airframe, and no
  known drawing set outside whatever Daher kept in Tarbes or Mooney kept in
  Kerrville. Daher's lineage controls everything TBM-derived; whether Mooney
  kept any right in the original M30 drawings is unknown.

**The TBM 700 TC, for comparison.** TCDS A60EU, Rev 40, 24 April 2026, status

the 20-page TCDS finds no mention of Mooney (FAA DRS, P).

## Holder or seller today

- **Mooney International Corporation** (Kerrville TX) is the successor to Mooney
  Aircraft Corporation, which built the 301. It has been owned by US Financial
  LLC since 2020 (see the M22 file for its status). No statement on the 301 was
  found.
- **Daher Aircraft SAS** holds the TBM type certificate and builds the TBM line
  (FAA DRS, P).
- **TBM International** was a joint venture formed in June 1987 between Mooney
  and Aérospatiale. Mooney held 30% (FundingUniverse company history, L/S).
  **Exit:** "by the summer of 1989 Mooney had been dropped from the partnership"
  (Mooney Events, L/S); "in 1991, suffering from a shortage of money ... Mooney
  pulled out" (AIN 2016, S). Best reading: sidelined in 1989, formal exit in
  1991. Both quotes re-read 2026-10-24.
- **Support:** none. There is no fleet.

## Drawings, molds and tooling

- **1987:** "all the engineering data for the M30 was delivered to the engineers
  in Tarbes", and the prototype was dismantled and shipped there (Mooney Events,
  L/S). Plane & Pilot says the design was transferred to Tarbes (S).
- **Later:** the prototype came back to Kerrville and sat in engineering for
  years. After the lawsuits settled it was scrapped: wings cut off, the rest
  given to an A&P school in Abilene, Texas (Mooney Events, citing Tom Bowen of
  Mooney, L/S; Wikipedia, L). Which school, and whether anything survives, is
  unknown.
- **Whether Mooney kept a copy of the drawings is unknown.**

**Status: unknown.** There is probably no airframe left to measure.

**Reading.** The data physically went to Socata, Socata built the TBM's flight
controls from it, and the only surviving TC in the lineage is Daher's. Mooney
may have kept copies and may have settled some right in them; no public record
says. Either way, what is left of the 301 itself is 40-year-old drawings for
an aircraft that flew 200 lb overweight, with no airframe to check them
against. The realistic route is a new design that takes the 301's layout as a
reference, with counsel on how close is too close to TBM-protected data.

## Patents and trademarks

- **Patents.** None identified. A 1983 patent would have expired by about 2000
  under the 17-year rule.
- **"MOONEY"**, Reg. 935574: live, owned by Mooney International Corp. (USPTO

- **"TBM 700"**, Reg. 1655496, Class 12: live, renewed January 2022, owned by
  Daher Aerospace (USPTO TSDR, P).
- **"301"** as a mark was not searched. A revival could not use "Mooney".

## Fleet

- FAA registry, 2026-10-24: make MOONEY model 301 returns **0**. Make MOONEY
  model M30 returns one model code (5872030, turboprop, 6 seats) with **0
  aircraft** (P). Positive control for the same query shape: make MOONEY model
  M22 returns 14, re-run the same day (P).
- Deregistered: N301MX, Mooney M30, s/n 0000, cancelled 26 July 1989 (P). The
  registry codes it as a turboprop, though the 301 flew with a piston engine.
  That is a registry coding oddity.
- **Built:** 1 prototype. It first flew on 7 April 1983 (Mooney Events, Plane &
  Pilot) or 21 April 1983 (Plane & Pilot 2021). About 70 hours of flying (L).

## Why it died

The prototype was about 200 lb over weight, and some aerodynamic work was
unfinished, so it was slower than planned. In July 1984 LTV bought Republic Steel and ordered it to sell
Mooney. The new French owners wanted close to 300 kt, which needed a turbine.
They moved the effort into a joint venture with Socata that became the TBM 700
(Mooney Events, L/S; Plane & Pilot, S).

## Where an aviation IP attorney is needed

1. **Who owns the M30 engineering data.** It was handed to Socata in 1987 under a
   joint venture whose terms are not public, then disputed in litigation that
   settled. Mooney International and Daher may both claim it.
2. **How far the TBM 700 and its TC carry rights in the 301's features**, such as
   the Fowler flap and spoiler roll-control layout.
3. **Trade secret exposure** if anyone with access to Mooney or Socata data joins
   a revival.
