# Meyers 200D / Aero Commander 200D — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon (census legwork
delegated, judgement mine). **Board issue:** AER-3787. **Date:** 2026-10-24.
**Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only. Nobody has been
contacted. From AERO's stage 1 long list (near miss: speed unconditioned, useful
load 100 lb short).

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **The TC is live and has a small holder.** TCDS 3A18 (Models 200 to 200D, plus
  the Interceptor 400 turboprop) is "Current", sub-status empty, Rev 19, dated
  31 July 2014 (FAA DRS, P).
- **The holder name differs between DRS fields.** The metadata says "Interceptor
  Aircraft Inc". The PDF holder line says "Interceptor Aviation Inc., 6900 NW 63rd
  Street, Suite 100, Bethany, Oklahoma". The ownership record says the name
  changed to Interceptor Aviation, Inc. (Augusta, KS) on 3 April 2014 (P).
- **The TC, drawings, jigs and tooling were offered for sale in October 2019.**
  Interceptor Aviation's broker, AvStrategies, marketed the package. It
  included the Meyers 200 tooling and the one remaining Interceptor 400 (AOPA,
  Flying, AIN; S). No sale was found, and DRS shows no transfer since 2014.
- **The parent company has since changed hands.** GlobalParts Group formed
  Interceptor Aviation in 2014. VSE Corporation bought GlobalParts in July 2021
  for about $38 million (SEC filing, P). VSE's filings do not mention Interceptor
  or Meyers. **Whether Interceptor Aviation went with it is unknown.**
- **There is no production basis.** Every aircraft needs individual FAA
  conformity inspection (P).
- **Fleet:** 58 200D registered (P); about 83 to 85 built.
- **This is the batch's strongest buy case.** A holder that has already tried to
  sell the TC and tooling together is rare. Whoever owns it now has to be
  established first.

## Holder or seller today

- **Interceptor Aviation Inc.** was formed by GlobalParts Group (Augusta, KS)
  after it bought "the type certificate, intellectual property, tooling and
  assets of the Meyers Aircraft Model 200 type design" on 31 March 2014
  (GlobalParts release via AviationPros; AvWeb; S). GlobalParts promised parts
  and support for about 100 flying Meyers 200s (S).
- **Bethany, Oklahoma** is where GlobalParts bought Tulakes Aero in July 2014 (S).
  That explains the TCDS address.
- **2015:** GlobalParts said it was evaluating "limited production" and ran a
  Meyers 200 air-racing team (AvWeb, 13 August 2015; S).
- **October 2019:** the TC, drawings, jigs, tooling and an Interceptor 400 were
  marketed by Brian Foley of AvStrategies for Interceptor Aviation. The aircraft
  "has not flown for 13 years" and was parked in Wichita (AOPA, 1 November 2019;
  Flying, 28 October 2019; S).
- **July 2021:** VSE Corporation acquired Global Parts Group, Inc. (SEC 8-K,
  P). Global Parts merged into VSE Aviation, Inc. in December 2022 (S). VSE's
  8-K and Q3 2021 10-Q do not mention Interceptor or Meyers (P).
- **Corporate status of Interceptor Aviation Inc.:** not checked in the Kansas or
  Oklahoma Secretary of State records. **Open item.**
- **Meyers Aircraft Co. revival:** the 1990s "New Meyers Aircraft" (later Micco
  Aircraft, Fort Pierce, FL) revived the Meyers 145 (TCDS 3A1, now holder MICCO
  Aircraft Company), not the 200 (Aviation Consumer; FAA DRS; S/P). It has no
  bearing on 3A18.

## Drawings, molds and tooling

- **Both 2014 and 2019 sources say the tooling and drawings exist** and went with
  the TC. The 2019 package was "carefully stored in obscurity" in Kansas
  (Flying, S).
- The original Meyers airframe was largely hand-built in 4130 steel with little
  tooling (Plane & Pilot 2024, S). So "tooling" probably means jigs and fixtures,
  not composite molds.
- **Where they are now:** unknown. They were in Kansas in 2019.

## Patents and trademarks

- **Patents.** 1950s and 1960s patents expired decades ago. No live patents are
  expected. Not searched.
- **"MEYERS"** for aircraft: one filing by Prop-Jets, Inc. (SN 75211677),
  **abandoned**. "MEYERS" is live for other goods, such as Meyers Manx vehicles
  (USPTO, P).
- **"INTERCEPTOR"** for aircraft: several filings by unrelated parties (Insitu,
  Eipper, Minnesota Multitask), all dead. **No Interceptor Aviation filing found**
  (P). Positive control: the same search for "MOONEY" returns the live Reg.
  935574.

## Fleet

- FAA releasable aircraft database, downloaded 2026-10-24 (P):
  - AERO COMMANDER 200D: 54 (serials 297 to 382, 1966-1967)
  - MEYERS INDUSTRIES INC 200D: 4 (serials 290 to 294, 1964-1965)
  - **Total 58.** For context, the other 200-series models are on separate
    registry codes. The 2014 sources say about 100 Meyers 200s of all models fly.
- **Built:** Plane & Pilot gives 124 Meyers 200s of all models: 49 Meyers-built
  and 75 Aero Commander 200D. Wikipedia gives 8 Meyers-built 200Ds and 77 Aero
  Commanders. So about 83 to 85 200Ds were built (S/L).

## Why it died

The 200 was hand-built with little tooling. Rockwell's Aero Commander division
bought the rights in 1966. It "spent US$4 million to produce just US$3 million
worth of product" and stopped in 1968 (Wikipedia, L; Plane & Pilot 2024, S). The
rights then passed to Interceptor Corporation. Interceptor put its money into the
turboprop 400, which was certified in 1971 but never financed for production (L).
