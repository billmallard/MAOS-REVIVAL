# Mooney M22 Mustang — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon (census legwork
delegated, judgement mine). **Board issue:** AER-3787. **Date:** 2026-10-24.
**Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only. Nobody has been
contacted. Fed by AERO, stage 1 batch 1.

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **The TC is live on paper, but its holder line has not changed since 1973.** TCDS
  A6SW (M22 only) shows status "Current", sub-status empty, Rev 6 of 1 December
  1973, holder "Mooney Aircraft Corporation, Kerrville, Texas". The M20 family's TC
  (2A3) records every holder transfer since 1969, ending with Mooney International
  Corporation in 2013. **A6SW records none.**
- **Who holds A6SW today is not shown by any public record found.** It may have
  passed to Mooney International with the other assets without a TCDS update. Or
  it may be effectively orphaned. This is the single most important open fact in
  the batch, and only the FAA's records or counsel can settle it.
- **There is no production basis.** The TCDS says "Production basis: None", so
  each aircraft built after March 1969 needed individual FAA conformity
  inspection.
- **The manufacturer does not support the type.** Mooney's technical-publications
  library explicitly excludes the M22. Its only M22 manuals are a 1967 POH and
  *preliminary* maintenance and parts manuals. Owners make or salvage parts.
- **The fleet is tiny:** 14 registered, 10 with standard airworthiness
  certificates, "a handful" flying.
- **The name is not available.** "MUSTANG" for aircraft is a live Textron mark
  (Citation Mustang). "MOONEY" is a live Mooney International mark.
- **This is the batch's best case for a purchase or licence:** a small holder, a
  type it does not support, and a TC record nobody has touched in 53 years. That
  is a reason to ask Bill at the shortlist stage, not now (see the end of this
  file).

## The holder today

- **Ownership.** US Financial, LLC has owned Mooney International since September
  2020, with Jonny Pollack as CEO. It says support of the existing fleet (7,000+
  aircraft) comes first (Plane & Pilot, S; Wikipedia, L).
- **Mooney's website** (© 2026 Mooney International Corporation, Kerrville TX) says
  its service-centre directory and publications index were "recovered from a June
  2026 website archive" (P). That suggests a recent disruption. No bankruptcy or
  sale was found for 2021-2026; PACER and Texas Secretary of State filings were not
  searched.
- **June 2026:** a bid for Spirit Airlines under the name "Mooney International"
  was reported. The Kerrville company reportedly denied any link (S/L). This is
  noise for the rights question, but it shows the name is being used by others.
- **Support for the M22: effectively none.**
  - Mooney's tech-pubs list (P) shows only POH-M22 (1967), a preliminary service
    and maintenance manual MAN205 (1977) and a preliminary parts catalogue
    68-22-PC-P (1968).
  - The master technical publications library is labelled "M20 thru M20TN
    (EXCLUDING M22)".
  - The Mooney Flyer (August 2024; S): "no longer supported by the manufacturer".

## Drawings and tooling

- **No public statement on whether M22 drawings or tooling survive**, or who holds
  them, from Mooney, the Mooney Aircraft Pilots Association or a museum.
- **UT Dallas History of Aviation Archives** holds Al Mooney's papers, with some
  drawings digitised (M-19 three-views seen). No M22 items were found (P).
- **Museum aircraft:**
  - MidAmerica Aircraft Museum Foundation (Liberal, KS) is the registered owner of
    N7727M, s/n 690002 (FAA registry, P).
  - One M22 is reported on display at the State Aviation Museum in Kyiv (L).

**Status: unknown.** Physical survivors exist and could be measured. That is a
re-derivation question, below.

## STCs and PMA

- 117 STCs name A6SW or the M22, all multi-model equipment STCs: avionics, engine
  monitors, lighting, speedbrakes, G100UL fuel. **None is structural or
  pressurisation-related, and none covers re-manufacture** (FAA DRS, P).
- 96 PMA records list the M22, all generic: brakes, rivets, avionics, ignition,
  drains. **No PMA covers M22-specific airframe or pressurisation parts** (P).

## Patents and trademarks

- **Patents.** No M22-specific patents were identified. US patents granted before
  8 June 1978 ran 17 years from grant, so any 1960s patent expired by the late
  1980s.
- **"MOONEY"**, Reg. 935574: live, Class 12 aircraft and parts, owned by Mooney
  International Corp., renewed 2022 (USPTO TSDR, P).
- **"MUSTANG"** for aircraft:
  - Reg. 822730 (Cavalier Aircraft Corp., filed 1965) expired in 1987.
  - Reg. 3098462, Class 12, owned by Textron Innovations Inc., is **live**
    (renewed May 2026) (USPTO TSDR, P).
  - Wikipedia says Cavalier sued Mooney over the name, which became "Mark 22" (L;
    the suit itself is unverified).
- A revival could use neither name.

## Fleet

- FAA registry, make/model MOONEY M22, 2026-10-24: **14 aircraft**, all with valid
  registration (P). By certificate:
  - 10 standard: 8 in the normal category, 2 with no category recorded
  - 2 special flight permit (dated 1973 and 1979)
  - 1 restricted (probably a data error)
  - 1 experimental exhibition (certificate dated February 2026)
  - Recounted from the ReleasableAircraft download of 2026-10-24
    (`CERTIFICATION` field: 8 x `1N`, 2 x `1`, 2 x `8..`, 1 x `31`, 1 x `43`).
    The first pass listed 15; AERO caught it.
- **Built:** about 31 production serials plus prototypes. The TCDS lists 25
  explicit serials plus "700001 and on", and Mooney's publications list shows 6
  in 1970. Other sources say 36, and some say the mid-20s.
- **Airworthy:** unknown. "Only a handful are flying" (Historynet, 2020; S).

## Why it died

Mooney sold each M22 at a loss, which contributed to its 1969 bankruptcy. The
TIO-541 had a 1,300 h TBO and a reputation for being touchy (Historynet 2020;
Plane & Pilot 2022; S). The engine was the weakness here too, which an
Aeromomentum re-engine would address. That is AERO's call.

## Safety notes from the record

DRS shows 7 ADs carrying the M22: 85-24-03 (fuel tank bays and rib stations,
shared with the M20), 67-26-04 (altimeters), and five multi-make appliance ADs. **No
pressurisation-specific AD was found** (P). The full NTSB history belongs in the
dossier, not here.

## Where an aviation IP attorney is needed

1. **Who legally holds TC A6SW.** It shows the 1973 holder name, with no recorded
   transfer to the Mooney successors that took the M20 TC. Did it pass with the
   assets in the 1969-2020 transfers, is it held by Mooney International without a
   TCDS update, or is it effectively orphaned? This also needs an FAA records
   request: that is a public-records request, not contact with a rights holder.
2. **The terms of any purchase or licence**, if Bill authorises an approach.
3. **Re-derivation from a measured airframe**, depending on the answer to 1.

**The abandonment route (49 USC 44704(a)(5); see `README.md`).** If the FAA,
using due diligence, could not find the owner of record or an heir, it could
designate A6SW abandoned and release the engineering data it holds. Here the
owner of record is a 1973 name whose likely successor, Mooney International,
exists and can be found. So this route would probably end with the FAA finding
an owner, not releasing data. Still, a request would force the holder question
into the open. Filing one is an outward act and Bill's call.
