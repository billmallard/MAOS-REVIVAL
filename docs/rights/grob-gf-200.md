# Grob GF 200 — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon (census legwork
delegated, judgement mine). **Board issue:** AER-3787. **Date:** 2026-10-24.
**Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only. Nobody has been
contacted. From AERO's stage 1 long list (incomplete: no fuel flow or empty
weight published).

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **No TC exists, at EASA or the FAA.** The GF 200 was a one-off technology
  demonstrator, first flown 26 November 1991. Grob TCs (G 115, G 120A, G 120TP,
  G 520) show up as positive controls in both databases (P).
- **The only aircraft is in a museum.** Prototype D-EFKH (c/n 20001) was
  photographed in the Deutsches Museum, Munich, in 2010 and seen there in 2015
  (L). Its current display status was not confirmed.
- **The company has changed hands twice since.** Grob Aerospace went insolvent
  in August 2008. H3 Aerospace bought "the training aircraft and support
  business" in January 2009 (Grob Aircraft AG, later SE). In June 2025 Helsing
  signed to buy Grob Aircraft SE (S; P, Grob press release).
- **Whether the GF 200 data went to H3 in 2009 is not shown publicly.** The 2009
  deal covered trainers and support. A shelved 1990s demonstrator may have stayed
  with the insolvency estate.
- **The design was not complete for production.** The prototype had no
  pressurisation, de-icing or full interior (Wikipedia, citing Flight; L/S). Grob
  then built a GF 250 to attract partners.
- **Any 1980s-90s patents have expired.** "GROB AIRCRAFT" is a live US mark now
  owned by Helsing GmbH (P).

## Holder or seller today

- **Grob Aerospace** (Mattsies/Tussenhausen) filed for insolvency on 18 August
  2008. The administrator was Dr Michael Jaffé. H3 Aerospace and Guizhou bid
  (Wikipedia, citing AIN and Aviation Week; L/S).
- **Grob Aircraft AG / SE**: H3 Aerospace bought the training aircraft and support
  business in January 2009. It builds the G 120TP and supports the G 115, G 120A
  and G 520 (FAA DRS holder lines, P). About 275 staff (Grob press release, P).
- **Helsing SE** signed a binding agreement to buy Grob Aircraft SE on 4 June 2025,
  subject to regulatory approval (Grob Aircraft press release, P). A later
  "completes acquisition" headline (Solicitors Journal, 6 June 2025) describes
  only the signing (S). **Completion was not confirmed** from a primary source.
  The USPTO record for "GROB AIRCRAFT" now names Helsing GmbH as owner (P), which
  suggests the deal closed.
- **Grob-Werke GmbH & Co. KG**, the family machine-tool company, is a separate
  business. It owns its own GROB marks (P).
- **Support for the GF 200: none.** There is no fleet.

## Drawings, molds and tooling

- **Unknown.** It is an all-composite design, including the propeller driveshaft
  (L). Molds for a single prototype may not have been kept.
- The airframe survives at the Deutsches Museum (L). Museum ownership of a
  physical object does not carry the design rights.
- GF 250 (1997, a more production-like variant) also existed (L). Its fate was
  not found.

## Patents and trademarks

- **Patents.** None identified. Any German or US patent filed for the GF 200 in
  1983-1997 would have expired (20 years from filing). Not searched in depth.
- **"GROB AIRCRAFT"**, SN 79446797, classes 9, 12, 41: **live**, owner shown as
  Helsing GmbH (USPTO trademark search, P).
- **"GROB AEROSPACE"**, SN 79045692: abandoned. **"GROB"** (Grob-Werke Burkhart
  Grob e.K.), SN 79022078, class 7/12 aircraft: cancelled (P).
- **Positive control:** the same search returns the live Textron "MUSTANG"
  aircraft mark, SN 76436781 (P).
- A revival could not use "Grob".

## Fleet

- FAA registry, make GROB, 2026-10-24: gliders, G 109, G 115 and G 520. **No GF
  200** (P). Control: MOONEY M22 returns 14 (P).
- **Built:** 1 (GF 200, D-EFKH), plus the GF 250 variant (L).

## Why it died

Grob could not find a partner to finance production. Flight International
reported "Grob seeks partner to finance GF200 launch" in November 1994. Early
flight tests also showed engine cooling, exhaust and noise problems (Flight
International 1992; Wikipedia; S/L). The GF 250 of 1997 did not attract a partner
either.
