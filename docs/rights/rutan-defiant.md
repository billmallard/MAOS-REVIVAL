# Rutan Defiant (Model 40) — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon (census legwork
delegated, judgement mine). **Board issue:** AER-3787. **Date:** 2026-10-24.
**Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only. Nobody has been
contacted. From AERO's stage 1 long list (pass). **Updated 2026-10-24** for
AERO's stage 3 question (rank 1): who bought the design rights in 1985, and what
does the holder own? See "The 1985 'sale' of rights" below.

**Model number.** Rutan's own memoir calls the design the "Model 40 Defiant"
(Ch24, P), and Hiller's page says "Defiant 40". "Model 74" traces only to Wikipedia (L); AERO
confirmed this on review and its stage 3 files now use Model 40.

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **Plans have not been sold since June 1985, and no new builder licence has
  been issued since.** Rutan Aircraft Factory (RAF) sold its last plans and
  "licensed its last builder" in June 1985 (Burt Rutan's memoir chapter on
  RAF, updated May 2025; S).
- **Each plans buyer got a licence to build one airplane.** Plans sold without
  that licence were for study only. RAF once allowed transfers between
  builders, but stopped in 1985 (Canard Pusher 54, January 1988; memoir; P).
- **No evidence that anyone bought the Defiant rights in 1985.** One sentence
  in Canard Pusher 54 (January 1988) says "since mid 1985 when rights to the RAF
  designs were sold". It is a reworded reprint of a Canard Pusher 46 paragraph
  (October 1985) that does not mention a sale. Every other RAF and Rutan account
  says RAF simply stopped selling plans and licences, and kept its assets. When
  a sale did happen (the Model 97 Microlite), the Canard Pushers name the buyer.
  None is named for the Defiant. **The holder today is most likely Rutan Aircraft
  Factory or Burt Rutan.** That is an inference from the record. No document
  says so.
- **What the holder owns, as far as the record shows:** the plans copyright
  (with a possible interest for Fred Keller, who drew the homebuilder plans for
  a share of sales), RAF's licensing role, and nothing more. The prototype
  belongs to the Hiller museum. The "Defiant" name is taken for aircraft by
  Sikorsky and Lockheed Martin. There is no type certificate and no live
  patent.
- **RAF stopped trading in 2002.** Its 109th and last Canard Pusher went out in
  2002. Support passed to builder groups (memoir, P). Corporate status was not
  verified.
- **Rutan still objects to unlicensed building.** In February 2025 he wrote that
  "some people are still building RAF homebuilt aircraft, without license and
  without approval from RAF, using bogus plans" (memoir, P). That is the
  holder's current, public attitude.
- **The prototype survives.** N78RA is at the Hiller Aviation Museum, San Carlos,
  California. Its FAA registration was cancelled in 2013, with the museum as
  last owner (FAA registry, P; Hiller, S).
- **The fleet is small:** 24 registered Defiants (FAA registry, P); 176 plans
  sets sold (Wikipedia, L).

## Holder or seller today

- **Nobody sells Defiant plans.** RAF stopped in June 1985 (memoir, P).
- **Why it stopped** (memoir, P):
  - Scaled Composites, founded 1982, needed Rutan's full attention.
  - Builder support was a heavy load, much of it for the least able builders.
  - Lawsuits after accidents. Product-liability insurance was "definitely not
    affordable".
- **RAF stayed open for support until 2002**, then "ceased being a business"
  (memoir, P). Tonya Rutan ran the office from 1989 to 2002.
- **Burt Rutan** lives in Coeur d'Alene, Idaho. He still registers an RAF-built
  aircraft (N187RR, Catbird) in his name, Elbert L. Rutan (FAA registry, P).
- **California corporate record: secondary only.** Bizapedia, which republishes
  California Secretary of State data, lists Rutan Aircraft Factory, Inc. as
  entity 972609, filed 14 January 1980, status "Suspended - FTB" since 1 April
  2009, agent Elbert L. Rutan, Mojave (S/L). California's own site refused
  automated access (HTTP 429), so this is not confirmed at source. A suspended
  California corporation cannot lawfully transact business until it is revived.
  Rutan's memoir says he "again activated RAF" after 2013 to develop the SkiGull
  (Ch19, P); if a newer RAF entity exists, perhaps in Idaho, it was not found.
- **Support today** comes from independent groups: the Canard Owners and
  Builders Association, and Rutan Aircraft Flying Experience (RAFE), Waller,
  Texas (memoir, P). RAFE is a separate registrant; it is not RAF (FAA
  registry, P).

## Drawings, molds and tooling

- **The Defiant used Rutan's moldless technique.** There are no production
  molds to inherit.
- **Drawings:** the homebuilder plans exist in builders' hands (176 sets sold).
  AOPA says the plans came from Fred Keller's build of the second prototype at
  Rutan's request (AOPA Pilot 2020, S). Who holds the master drawings is not
  public.
- **Prototype:** N78RA, s/n 001, first flown 30 June 1978. Rutan kept it as his
  own aircraft, then gave it to Hiller (Hiller, S). The FAA deregistration
  record names Hiller Aviation Museum, San Carlos CA, with a cancel date of
  9 May 2013 (FAA DEREG file, P). Hiller lists it on its site. Whether it is
  on display now was not confirmed.

## Kit or plans rights

- **Licence model.** Buying plans entitled the buyer to apply for a licence to
  build one airplane. The licensee "has the permission of RAF to copy the RAF
  prototype ... for one airframe" and is the manufacturer (Canard Pusher 54, S).
- **No new licences since 1985.** "We cannot issue further licenses for any
  further production of the designs" (Canard Pusher 54, S).
- **Resale of plans or projects.** RAF said a licensee who sells his plans or
  project is "effectively licensing the third party", should get an
  indemnity, and remains responsible for support (Canard Pusher 54, S).
- **Licences are closed for good.** "RAF cannot issue any new license
  agreements to anyone for any reason. This is final, official and
  irrevocable" (Canard Pusher 71, P). Canard Pusher 65 cancelled licences not
  started by 1 January 1991, later extended to 1 April 1991 (CP66, P).

### The 1985 "sale" of rights

The question from AERO's stage 3 was who bought the rights in 1985. The record
does not show a buyer, and on balance it shows there was none. Read from the
Canard Pusher mirror and Rutan's memoir, 2026-10-24:

| Source | What it says | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| CP45, 13 July 1985 | "After today, July 13, 1985, RAF will no longer market homebuilt aircraft plans." The remaining money funds builder support. No sale is mentioned. | P |
| CP46, October 1985 | "However since this summer, current agreements specify that RAF support only those who are previously licensed to build the RAF designs" | P |
| CP54, January 1988 | The same paragraph, reworded: "However since mid 1985 when rights to the RAF designs were sold agreements specify that RAF support only those who are previously licensed ... and we cannot issue further licenses" | P |
| CP55 | "instead of cashing in the RAF bank account when we decided to no longer market plans" | P |
| CP65 | "we discontinued the sale of plans and the licensing of individuals ... in mid-1985" | P |
| Memoir Ch19 (2025) | "In June, 1985 RAF sold its last set of plans, and licensed its last builder ... RAF did not liquidate assets." It reprints the CP46 wording, not CP54. | P |
| CP65, positive control | "Rights to the concept were later sold to another English Company, Aviation Composites" (the Model 97 Microlite). The newsletters do name a buyer when there is one. | P |

**Reading.** The CP54 clause most likely means that rights (licences) to build
the designs were sold to builders until mid 1985, or it is an editing slip. It
does not read as a transfer to a third party. That is my inference, not a
finding. Searched: CP44 to CP58 and CP60 to CP105 (CP59 is missing from the
mirror), memoir chapters 19 and 24. Not searched: Kitplanes, Sport Aviation,
AOPA archives, the canard forums.

**The drawings.** The homebuilder plans were not Rutan's 1977 prototype
drawings. Fred Keller built the second Defiant, photographed the process and
wrote new drawings; "I would sell them at RAF and pay him a portion of the
income" (memoir Ch24, P). Whether Keller kept any copyright in them is not
shown.

**Rutan's later stance.** Chapter 24 (2026) gives the Defiant's history and says
nothing about licensing or reviving it. Its one open offer is for the Boomerang:
"It is not patented and no license is needed from me" (P). The Defiant gets no
such offer, and chapter 19 objects to building "without license" (above).

## Patents and trademarks

- **Patents.** None found. Any 1978-85 US patent expired long ago.
- **"DEFIANT"** in Class 12 is taken for helicopters: Sikorsky's DEFIANT (SN
  87610238) and SB>1 DEFIANT, and Lockheed Martin's DEFIANT X (SN 90482320,
  90482354), all live (USPTO search index, P).
- **"RUTAN":** no live aircraft mark. Elbert Leander Rutan filed RUTAN for
  clothing and model airplanes in 2018 (SN 88214503); it was abandoned (P).
  "LONG-EZ" and "VARIEZE": no records (P).
- Positive control: the same search for "MOONEY" returns live SN 72336652,
  aircraft and parts (P).
- A revival could not use the DEFIANT name for aircraft. Using Rutan's name
  needs his agreement.

## Fleet

- FAA registry, ReleasableAircraft database downloaded 2026-10-24 (P):
  **24** current registrations with "DEFIANT" in the make or model, excluding
  one "MINI DEFIANT". Builder-entered names, so the count is approximate.
  Positive control: the same method returns 14 for the Mooney M22.
- AOPA (2020) estimates about 20 really flying; the FAA lists more because of
  misidentified entries (S). Nine were flying by mid-1987 (Wikipedia, L).
- **Plans sold:** 176 sets in 1984-85 (Wikipedia, L). Another source says about
  100 (L).

## Why it died

Rutan built the Defiant to prove a safe light twin that needs no pilot action
after an engine failure. Certification funding was not found in 1979, and the
big manufacturers ignored it. RAF sold plans for only a year before it stopped
all plans sales in June 1985. The reasons were Scaled Composites, the support
load and lawsuits (Wikipedia, L; AOPA 2020, S; memoir, P).
