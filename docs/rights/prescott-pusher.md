# Prescott Pusher — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon. **Board issue:**
AER-3805. **Date:** 2026-10-24. **Status:** first pass, desk research from
public sources only. Nobody has been contacted. Bill: "possibly the top of my

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **No type certificate. A kit, built to Part 23 standards.** Three of the four
  registered examples are experimental; the fourth, N40LE, has no
  classification on record (FAA registry, P). Wikipedia says it "was designed to
  FAR Part 23 certification standards of the time" (L, citing Sport Aviation,
  March 1986).
- **The engineering record survives, in a public archive.** The San Diego Air &
  Space Museum holds the **Prescott Aeronautical Corporation Collection,
  SC.10269**, donated in 2021 and "open for research with no restrictions"
  (SDASM finding guide, December 2022, P). Its box listing includes:
  - the general assembly drawing (10001) and well over a hundred part and
    installation drawings: fuselage, wing structure, spars and ribs, empennage,
    landing gear, engine mount, engine installation, **the propeller extension
    (15302)** and cooling baffles;
  - "Loads-basic load report", wind tunnel evaluation, weight and balance loading
    schedule, "Tradeoff of load factor vs weight";
  - structural test reports for the wing (static test, 16 September 1986),
    stabiliser, elevator, rudder, vertical tail, flaps, ailerons and engine mount,
    plus landing-gear drop tests and fail-safe data;
  - the Pilot Operations Manual and the build manuals for all kits, 101 to 131;
  - original tooling drawings and tool lists;
  - a folder titled "Bill of Sale" in the corporate records.
- **Nothing like it exists for any other candidate in this survey.** For the
  Rohr 2-175 the drawings were destroyed. For the Prescott, the drawings, the
  loads and the test reports sit in a museum box listing.
- **Who owns the design rights is not known.** The company stopped trading in
  1989 (Wings Over Kansas, S) or 1991 (SDASM, P). No buyer of its rights, and no
  bankruptcy record, has been found yet. Custody of the papers (SDASM) is not
  the same as ownership of the copyright in them, and the finding guide itself
  warns: "Some materials may be subject to copyright restrictions" (P).

## Type certificate

None. Amateur-built kit. The prototype, N41PP (s/n 001, 1985), lists
manufacturer PRESCOTT AERONAUTICAL and is **registered to the Kansas Aviation
Museum, Wichita**. Its category is Experimental, Market Survey / Research and
Development, with an airworthiness date of 24 September 1986 (FAA registry, P).
So the museum's displayed Pusher is the prototype.

## Holder or seller today

- **Prescott Aeronautical Corporation**, Wichita, Kansas. Founded by Tom Prescott
  and Stan Blankenship in 1980 (SDASM, P) or 1983 (Wikipedia; Wings Over
  Kansas). Linden Blue, the former Beech CEO, was an investor and officer
  (Wikipedia; airplanezone, L).
- **End:** "In 1989, having produced 68 kits, Prescott Aeronautical Corporation
  went out of business" (Wikipedia, citing Wings Over Kansas, L). Wings Over
  Kansas: existed 1983 through 1989, "Manufactured 68 kits/3 aircraft" (S).
  SDASM: "in 1991 the company folded as builders were still completing their
  aircraft" (P).
- **Corporate status:** Kansas Secretary of State records not searched (no open
  data found). **Open item.**
- **Successor:** none found. A secondary search summary named a successor sales
  company and Prescott's estate as possible holders; I could not find the
  primary source, so it is not relied on here.
- **The SDASM "Bill of Sale" folder** (Box 1, Series I, Folder 5) is the most
  direct lead on where the assets went. Reading it means visiting the archive or
  asking SDASM for a copy. That is contact with a museum, so it is Bill's call
  (see the index's Contact section).

## Drawings, molds and tooling

- **Drawings: survive, at SDASM.** The listing is drawing-number by
  drawing-number. Series V alone runs to 185 folders (Box 3 folders 1-86, Box 4 folders 1-99). Series II has
  installation, engineering and original tooling drawings.
- **The design was CAD/CAM from the start.** The Pusher was "the first aircraft
  in its class totally designed using CAD/CAM" (SDASM, P). The archive holds
  paper. Whether the original CAD files survive anywhere is unknown.
- **Tooling:** original tooling drawings and tool lists survive at SDASM. The
  physical tooling's fate after 1989 is unknown.
- **Molds:** the fuselage is a 4130 steel tube frame with composite skins
  (Wikipedia, L). The finding guide lists drawings for the fuselage shells
  (11040, 11041) and "Precision composites- chris lair" (Folder 33), probably
  the composite supplier. Whether shell molds survive is unknown.

## Kit or plans rights

The kit was sold in 33 sub-kits, each with a manual and a video
(airplanezone, L). SDASM holds the manuals for kits 101-131 (P). The
videotapes were "removed from this collection and transferred to the Audio
Visual Collections", so they are still at SDASM but not in SC.10269 (finding
guide, P). No one has sold the kit since the company closed. The builders' rights to
complete and maintain their own aircraft are not in question. The right to sell
new kits or plans is the open one.

**Type club or builder group: none found** (Bill asked for this on AER-3805;
identify only, no contact). Probes, 2026-10-24:
- web searches for a Prescott Pusher owners' or builders' group, including on
  groups.io and Yahoo Groups: nothing;
- the Homebuilt Homepage clubs list: the page is gone (404);
- a Pilots of America thread (April 2021): owners' and onlookers' discussion of
  one aircraft for sale. It is not a group and holds no plans (L).

The likeliest holders of builder documentation are individual builders and the
SDASM collection itself. Separately, an R/C 1/5-scale model plan of the Pusher
is published on Outerzone. That is a model plan, not the aircraft's drawings
(L).

## Fleet

- FAA registry, 2026-10-24 (P), records whose make or model names the Prescott
  Pusher: **4**. N41PP (s/n 001, the prototype, Kansas Aviation Museum, experimental R&D), N10JS (s/n 008,
  California, airworthiness 1991), N200SX (s/n 100, Florida, 1996) and N40LE
  (s/n 32, Illinois, classification "Unknown", no airworthiness date).
- **This is a lower bound.** Amateur-built aircraft are registered under the
  builder's name and model string. AERO is doing the full survivor count.
- **Built:** 68 kits sold (Wings Over Kansas, S). By 1991 there were "approximately
  25-35 builders completing their aircraft" (Wikipedia, citing Sport Aviation,
  September 1991, L).

## Why it died

On the record: the company closed while builders were still mid-kit (SDASM, P).
One retrospective blames "the negative buzz generated after some early
completions", with reports of "high takeoff (rotation) speeds, pilot induced
oscillations on takeoff, and uncommanded rolls on takeoff" (airplanezone, L).
AERO owns the full "why it died" question.

## Where an aviation IP attorney is needed

1. **Who owns the copyright in Prescott Aeronautical's drawings and manuals**
   now that the corporation is gone. That turns on whether it was dissolved,
   sold its assets (the "Bill of Sale" folder), or went bankrupt, and in which
   state.
2. **What SDASM's custody means.** The finding guide says "Copyright: SDASM"
   in its cataloguing block, and "Some materials may be subject to copyright
   restrictions" (P). Did the 2021 donor have the rights to transfer, and did
   they? What use does SDASM permit of copies?
3. **Using the data for a new design.** If the copyright owner cannot be found,
   what may a new design take from the loads reports and structural test results
   (facts and data) as opposed to the drawings themselves (expression)? Can the
   general arrangement be re-drawn from first principles?
4. **The "Prescott Pusher" name.**
