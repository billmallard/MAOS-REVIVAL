# Rohr 2-175 (71X) — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon. **Board issue:** AER-3787.
**Date:** 2026-10-24. **Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only.
Nobody has been contacted.

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

## Summary

- **Rohr did not go out of business.** Rohr, Inc. still exists as a wholly owned
  unit of Collins Aerospace (RTX). Any rights that survive from the 2-175 program
  sit with that corporate chain, not with nobody.
- **There is very little left to own.** There is no type certificate: FAA DRS holds
  none for Rohr or the 2-175. Both flying
  prototypes, the static test article and the tooling were reportedly cut up and
  scrapped when the program ended, and a former Rohr employee states the
  documentation was destroyed too. None of this is confirmed from a primary record.
- **The one patent found has expired** (US 3,931,943, expired 1993).
- **No airframe survives, so it cannot be re-derived from one.** What exists
  publicly is photographs, a 3-view, a handful of specifications, and an
  out-of-print tribute book.
- **As built, it is far below the mission.** Two seats, 150 hp, 1,450 lb gross. It
  cannot carry a 1,000 lb useful load. A "revival" would be a new design that
  borrows its configuration ideas: delta planform, buried engine, ducted pusher
  fan, folding wings. That screen call is AERO's (stage 1); it is noted here
  because it decides whether the rights question matters.

**Dating conflict.** A homebuiltairplanes.com poster who says he worked with
Mooney dates the cancellation to 1973 and blames General Dynamics' losses on the
Washington subway. That cannot be right: the aircraft first flew in October 1974,
the patent was filed in August 1974, and the subway car contract was Rohr's, not
General Dynamics'. The rest of that account (prototypes and tooling scrapped, the
designer's offer to take the program private refused) agrees with the other
sources, but it is one unverified voice.

## Type certificate

- **No type certificate exists in FAA records.** The program flew two prototypes
  for 23 hours and was cancelled before production; no source mentions a TC
  application.
- **FAA registry, make/model table:** a search on manufacturer `ROHR` returns the
  1946 Rohr Aircraft Corporation MR-1 (positive control: the table does carry
  Rohr's own aircraft) but **no 2-175 entry**.
- **N62R today** is a reserved N-number held by a short-N-number broker since
  2016. The online registry carries no history of the 1974 aircraft; registry
  records that old are not online.
- **FAA DRS, the primary TCDS database, holds no Rohr TCDS** (read 2026-10-24,
  guest session; method in `README.md` here).
  - Positive controls: holder "Mooney Aircraft Corporation" returns TCDS A6SW, and
    model "M22" returns exactly that one record.
  - Neither "Rohr" nor model "2-175" appears in DRS's TC Holder list (779 values),
    its Former TC Holders list (634 values) or its Model list (8,068 values).
    Filters on holder "Rohr Industries" and on model "2-175" both return 0.
  - A keyword search for "Rohr" returns 25 TCDS. All are other makers' sheets that
    mention Rohr components (DC-10, A340, CF6 and others).
  - Limit: this rules out an issued TC that DRS carries. It cannot rule out an
    application that was never granted.

**Status: no type certificate (never certificated).**

## Drawings and tooling

- **Prototypes and tooling: reported destroyed.** Several independent secondary
  sources say Rohr cut up both flying prototypes, the static article and the
  tooling and scrapped them, to keep the design and technology private.
- **Drawings: reported destroyed.** A former Rohr employee who built a 3-D
  reconstruction of the aircraft (Flickr, "Michael VH") states that all
  documentation was destroyed. One voice, not confirmed.
- **What might survive, in public archives:**
  - **San Diego Air & Space Museum** holds the Rohr/Goodrich corporate collection,
    donated 2013: about 35,000 photographs and 135,000 slides and negatives, in 51
    boxes, with an online finding guide. A 2024 federal NHPRC grant funds
    cataloguing and digitising 170,000 of those images over two years, so
    program photographs may appear in the public catalogue during 2026. Its
    other Rohr collection reached (SC.10119, payroll and stock records) has nothing
    on the program.
  - **Academy of Model Aeronautics** holds an oral-history biography of Walt
    Mooney.
  - **Richard Fraser, "Rohr 2-175 (71-X) Fan Jet: A Tribute"**, an out-of-print
    book. The author's company website (fraseraerotechnologycompany.com) has lapsed
    and now serves unrelated content. Used copies have surfaced in the UK.
- Mooney's personal papers and estate: nothing found.

**Status: presumed destroyed; photographs probably survive at SDASM; no
engineering drawings known to survive.**

## Kit or plans rights

None. The aircraft never reached production, kit or plans sale. (A 36-inch
radio-control model plan circulates among modellers; it has no bearing on the
full-scale design.)

## Patents and trademarks

- **US 3,931,943, "Airplane rudder and controls"**, inventors Don M. Westergren and
  Walter E. Mooney, assignee Rohr Industries. Filed 30 August 1974, granted 13
  January 1976. A combined nose-wheel shroud and rudder for a low-cost light
  aircraft, evidently from this program. **Expired 1993.**
- **No other program patents found.** Any US patent from a 1974-76 program has
  expired regardless.
- **Trademarks:** "ROHR" is a corporate name of the Goodrich/Collins chain, and
  Goodrich filed USPTO marks after the 1997 acquisition. A full USPTO search for
  "ROHR", "2-175" and "71X" was **not run** here; it is the next step. Whatever it
  shows, a revival should not carry the Rohr name.

## Corporate succession

Rohr Aircraft Corporation (1940) → Rohr Corporation (1969) → Rohr Industries
(1971, the program's sponsor) → Rohr, Inc. (1992) → acquired by BFGoodrich
(1997) → Goodrich acquired by United Technologies (2012, UTC Aerospace Systems) →
Collins Aerospace (2018) → RTX (2020). Rohr, Inc. remains a wholly owned unit of
Collins Aerospace. Sources: SDASM finding aid; Wikipedia.

## Where an aviation IP attorney is needed

1. **Residual rights of the Rohr/Collins chain.** Whether any copyright in
   surviving drawings or documents, or any trade-secret claim, survives with
   Collins Aerospace, and what it would cover. Unpublished corporate drawings made
   before 1978 can remain in copyright for a long time; whether that reaches a new
   design drawn from photographs and published dimensions is a legal question.
2. **Name and trade dress.** Whether any live mark or trade-dress claim reaches the
   2-175's name or appearance. Do not use "Rohr" in any public material without
   counsel.
3. **Publishing a re-derived design** under AeroCommons' open licence, if that
   path is ever chosen.
