# RDD Enterprises LX7 (re-winged Lancair IV-P) — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon. **Board issue:**
AER-3805. **Date:** 2026-10-24. **Status:** desk research from public sources
only. Nobody has been contacted.

**Why this file exists.** Bill recalled "a company in Oregon" that re-wings
Lancairs and remanufactures the whole aircraft: "quite impressive actually, but
it's not cheap" (AER-3783). AERO will use it as an idea and a data point for the
IV-P dossier. This file identifies the company, records what it does and
charges, and says whether it holds any design rights. The parent design's file
is [`lancair-iv-p.md`](lancair-iv-p.md).

This is research, not legal advice.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator). Company pages are graded P for what the
company says about itself, not for whether its performance claims are true.

## Summary

- **The company is RDD Enterprises, LLC of Redmond, Oregon.** Oregon domestic
  LLC, registry 38977492, registered 18 October 2006; principal place of
  business 2244 SE Airport Way, Suite 130, Redmond (Oregon SOS, P). Co-owners
  David McRae (sales, marketing, business development) and Eric Schmidlin
  (engineering, manufacturing), per its leadership page (P). It has "been
  providing expert ownership assistance for high-performance personal aircraft
  (certified and experimental) since 2006" (rddent.com, P).
- **What it does.** "RDD finds existing Lancair IV-P aircraft with valid
  operating certificates, takes them in house, cuts off the tail, removes the
  wing, strips all the systems out (including engine, landing gear and
  instrument panel) and starts fresh" (AvWeb, 23 July 2019, S). The result is the
  **LX7**: new wing, new tail, de-ice, a whole-aircraft parachute, pressurised.
- **What it charges (published).**
  - 2016 launch: the conversion "for just $550,000", trading in an old IV-P
    (RDD release, 5 April 2016, P). The release frames it as a trade-in of the
    owner's IV-P, so the airframe is on top of that price.
  - 2019: $980,000 for the 550 hp PT6A turboprop and $821,000 for the
    Continental TSIO-550-E piston, complete (AvWeb, S). Flying's July 2019
    preview called it "a sky-high price tag", but its body text is paywalled;
    the price figures on Flying's pages sit only in sponsored "Key takeaways"
    summary boxes and are not used here.
  - 2023: the **LX7-1 kit**, "for an introductory price of less than $500,000",
    an initial construction package with carbon-fibre fuselage structures and a
    dual-spar one-piece wing; the owner finishes the build (RDD release via
    Airframer, 27 July 2023, P).
  - Current site: the turboprop at "an expected completed aircraft price of
    under $900,000" (rddent.com/lx7, © 2024, P). No 2025-2026 price list found.
- **Design rights: none claimed, none needed for what it does.** RDD works on
  amateur-built airframes that its customers already own. No public source shows
  a licence from, or a purchase of rights from, any Lancair entity. The wing and
  tail are RDD's own design. The LX7-1 kit includes "carbon fiber fuselage
  structures" (2023 release), which means RDD now makes fuselage parts itself.
  **Whether those parts derive from Lancair tooling or drawings, and whether that
  needs a Lancair licence, is not public.** That is the one rights question this
  file leaves open.

## What changes, in numbers (published)

From RDD's current LX7 pages (lx7aircraft.com, © 2025, P). AERO owns the
comparison against the stock IV-P; these are the inputs.

| | LX7 piston | LX7 turboprop | LX7-1 kit (2023, non-pressurised) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engine | Continental TSIO-550-E, 350 hp | PT6, 550-750 shp | owner's choice |
| Wing span / length | 34 ft / 25 ft | 34 ft / 25 ft | — |
| Wing area | 98 sq ft, unchanged from the IV-P (AvWeb 2019) | same | — |
| Empty weight | 2,350 lb | 2,250 lb | 2,100 lb "expected" |
| Max gross | 3,750 lb | 3,850 lb | 3,750 lb |
| Useful load (computed: gross minus empty) | 1,400 lb | 1,600 lb | 1,650 lb |
| Fuel | 180 US gal | 180 US gal | 125 US gal |
| Payload with full fuel (as published) | 320 lb | 294 lb | 900 lb |
| Max cruise | 260 KTAS at FL250, 24 gph | 315 KTAS at FL250, 40 gph | — |
| Economy cruise | 250 KTAS at FL250, 18 gph | 280 KTAS, 30 gph | — |
| Range | 1,690 nm at max cruise, 2,250 nm at economy | 1,300 nm at max cruise | — |
| Stall, Vso | 63 KIAS | 58 KIAS | — |
| Cabin differential | 5 psid | 5 psid | none |

The idea worth keeping, in RDD's own words: the new wing has "the same area as
the old, just 98 square feet, but the airfoil is new, the leading edge is created
from a cuff that helps ensure uniformity and it comes fitted with double-slotted
flaps. RDD's goals were to dramatically improve the IV-P's stall behavior and
reduce stall (and therefore approach) speeds" (AvWeb 2019, S). The IV-P's stall
and accident record is the reason it died (see the IV-P file). RDD's answer is
to keep the fuselage and replace the lifting surfaces.

**Caveats.** These are manufacturer figures, not tests. The 2,250 nm economy
range is at 18 gph with 180 gal, which is 10 hours, with no reserve stated on
that line. The 2019 AvWeb piece says 260 KTAS piston and 285 KTAS turboprop at
20,000 ft. The current site says 315 KTAS turboprop at FL250. The figures moved
between sources.

## Fleet

- RDD said in 2019 that it "will soon have eight of its 'conversions' flying and
  has the capacity of up to 10 LX7s a year" (AvWeb, S).
- FAA registry, 2026-10-24 (P): three aircraft carry an LX7 model string: N17LX
  (LIV-351, 2019), N77VY (LIV-248) and N222MA (s/n 014, 2020), all
  experimental (42). RDD itself owns N71LX (s/n 101, experimental). The LIV
  serials are IV-P donor serials. **This is a lower bound.** A converted
  airframe can stay registered as a Lancair, so the true count is not readable
  from the registry.
- 2022: RDD reported "a two-year backlog of work" after Oshkosh (rddent.com
  news, P).

## What this means for a revival

- **As an idea:** proof that the IV-P fuselage is worth keeping and the wing is
  what to replace. The market paid $0.8-1.0 million for that in 2019.
- **As a rights pattern:** modifying airframes that owners already hold needs no
  type-design licence, because the IV-P is amateur-built and has no TC. That is
  the "modify existing airframes" path in the IV-P file, done at scale by a
  real company. Its limit is the donor pool: about 235 registered IV-Ps.
- **As a cost data point:** the opposite of our mission. It is a premium
  remanufacture, and its price is set by labour on a one-off airframe.

## Where an aviation IP attorney is needed

1. **Fuselage structures in the LX7-1 kit.** If RDD makes new IV-P-derived
   fuselage parts, did it need, or get, a licence from the IV-P design holder?
   The answer is a precedent for how far a third party can go with a kit design
   it does not own.
