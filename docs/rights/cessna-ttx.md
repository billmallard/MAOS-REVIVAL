This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **One TC covers the whole family.** TCDS A00003SE covers the LC40-550FG
  (Columbia 300), LC42-550FG (Columbia 350), LC41-550FG (Columbia 400, Cessna
  400, Corvalis TT) and T240 (TTx). Status "Current", sub-status empty, Rev 27 of
  22 June 2016. Holder: Textron Aviation Inc., Wichita (FAA DRS, P).
- **The holder chain is fully recorded.** Pacific Aviation Composites USA (1998)
  to The Lancair Company, Columbia Aircraft Manufacturing, Cessna Aircraft
  Company (2007), and Textron Aviation (29 July 2015). 26 versions sit in DRS (P).
- **The TTx is a separate model on that TC.** The T240 was approved on 23
  January 2013, with a TSIO-550-C at 310 hp, built under Production Certificate
  PC-4 (P).
- **Textron ended the TTx in early 2018** on weak sales: 44 shipped at the 2015
  peak, 23 in 2017, against 355 Cirrus singles (AOPA, AvWeb; S).
- **The holder is one of the largest GA manufacturers.** It says it supports
  its existing fleet "across all platforms" (S). Nothing suggests it would sell.
- **The names are free on the register.** TTX (Reg. 4848936), CORVALIS, CESSNA
  CORVALIS TT and CESSNA CORVALIS TTX were all cancelled under Section 8
  (USPTO, P). "CESSNA" itself was not checked and is certainly not free.
- **Fleet:** 136 T240 and 473 LC41-550FG registered (P).

## Holder or seller today

- **Textron Aviation Inc.**, a Textron Inc. subsidiary. It is a large, active
  manufacturer (Cessna, Beechcraft) (P, TCDS address).
- **History.** Lance Neibauer's Lancair Company certified the LC40 in 1998 and
  renamed itself Columbia Aircraft. Columbia entered Chapter 11 in 2007. Cessna's
  $26.4 million auction bid was approved by the US Bankruptcy Court for the
  District of Oregon on 28 November 2007 (GA News, S; Wikipedia, L). Cessna moved
  production from Bend, Oregon to Independence, Kansas in 2009 (S).
- **Support.** At the 2018 cancellation Textron said it remained committed to
  "supporting our existing customer base across all platforms" (AvWeb, S). The
  TC is current and was revised as recently as 2016 (P).

## Kit or plans rights

None on this TC. The design descends from the Lancair ES kit, but the certified
aircraft has no kit rights attached. Whether any Lancair kit IP overlaps is not
covered here.

## Patents and trademarks

- **Patents.** Not searched in this pass. Columbia and Cessna patents filed
  2000-2013 could run as late as the early 2030s. **Open item.**
- **"TTX"** for aircraft, Reg. 4848936, Textron Innovations Inc.: **cancelled
  20 May 2022** (Section 8) (USPTO TSDR, P). Live TTX marks for railroad cars
  (TTX Company) and vehicle suspensions (Öhlins) remain in Class 12.
- **"CORVALIS"** (Reg. 3782180), **"CESSNA CORVALIS TT"** (Reg. 3869798) and
  **"CESSNA CORVALIS TTX"** (Reg. 4131656): all cancelled under Section 8 (P).
- **"COLUMBIA 350"** (Lancair Company): abandoned after an inter partes
  decision. No "COLUMBIA 400" aircraft mark was found. "COLUMBIA" is live in
  Class 12 for other goods (trucks, utility vehicles) (P).

## Why it died

Cessna bought the Columbia line in 2007 to counter Cirrus. It renamed the type
three times and moved production to Kansas, but sales never took off. TTx
shipments peaked at 44 in 2015 and fell to 23 in 2017, under 10 percent of
Cessna piston sales. Textron quietly ended production in January or February
2018 (AOPA, 21 February 2018; AvWeb, 20 February 2018; S).

## The paths

| Path | What it would take | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Buy the TC** | Buy A00003SE, which covers the T240 and the LC40/41/42 fleet, from Textron. | Unlikely. Textron supports about 760 registered aircraft on this TC and has no visible reason to sell. Requires contact; not done. |
| **License** | Licence T240 data from Textron. | Unlikely for the same reasons. Requires contact. |
| **Modify existing airframes** | STC an engine change onto existing TTx or Columbia 400 airframes. | Practical. About 600 LC41 and T240 airframes are registered. Composite repair rules in TCDS Note 3 apply. |
| **Re-derive from an airframe** | Measure a TTx and build a new design. | Physically possible. The design belongs to a large, active holder that supports it. **Attorney needed.** |

## Where an aviation IP attorney is needed

1. **Re-derivation** of a composite design held by Textron, including any
   Columbia or Cessna patents still in force.
2. **Use of "TTx" or "Corvalis"**: the registrations are dead, but residual
   common-law rights and Textron's "CESSNA" house mark are attorney questions.
3. **Lancair kit lineage**: whether Lancair ES kit IP has any bearing.
