# Lancair IV-P — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon (census legwork
delegated, judgement mine). **Board issue:** AER-3787. **Date:** 2026-10-24.
**Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only. Nobody has been
contacted. From AERO's stage 1 long list (pass).

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **No type certificate. The IV-P is a kit.** FAA DRS returns nothing for "IV-P"
  (P). The certified Lancair line (LC40/41/42, now Textron, TC A00003SE) is a
  different design.
- **The rights moved twice, and the holder today is identifiable.**
  - February 2017: Lancair International Inc. (Redmond, OR) sold all
    out-of-production kit models, including the IV and IV-P, with "all assets,
    intellectual designs, and ongoing support business", to Lancair International
    LLC of Uvalde, Texas (Mark and Conrad Huffstutler) (company release, P/S).
  - October 2023: JetEXE Aviation of Sacramento bought the Lancair kit business,
    reported to include older kit designs, molds and IP (AvWeb, Kitplanes, S).
- **The trading name today is Lancair Aerospace**, Sacramento Executive Airport,
  CEO Capt. Augustine Joseph. It sells new designs (Mako, Barracuda) and supports
  the IV, IV-P and IV-PT with service bulletins and parts (lancair.com, P/S).
- **"LANCAIR" is a live mark** (Reg. 7828166, 2025), owned jointly by JetEXE
  Aviation Corporation and Akyna Medical Management, LLC (USPTO TSDR, P).
- **The fleet is large for a kit:** about 235 IV-P and Propjet records on the FAA
  register, plus about 58 IV or unspecified (P). About 250 IV-Ps were flying by
  2011 (L).
- **"Mountain Aviation" did not show up** in any Lancair record found. The chain
  runs Oregon, then Texas, then California.
- **This is a real candidate for a licence or purchase**: a small, identifiable
  holder that does not sell IV-P kits. That is a question for Bill at the
  shortlist stage, not now.

## Holder or seller today

- **Lancair International Inc.** (Oregon corporation, registry 13271291,
  registered 2003-02-25, Redmond) is still listed active. Since 2022 it holds the
  assumed business name "Evolution Aircraft Company" (Oregon SOS open data, P). It
  kept only the Evolution line in 2017. Evolution reportedly stopped operating in
  late 2017 (AOPA via Wikipedia, L).
- **Lancair International, LLC** (Texas, SOS file 0802270082, chartered
  2015-08-11, 1 Sky View, Uvalde) is still listed active for franchise tax (Texas
  Comptroller open data, P). Whether it still holds anything after the 2023 sale
  is not shown.
- **JetEXE Aviation Corporation** (California) bought the business in October
  2023 and moved the parts division to Sacramento (AvWeb, Kitplanes, S). The site
  now trades as **Lancair Aerospace**, 6273 Freeport Blvd, Sacramento, © 2026
  (lancair.com, P/S). California SOS records were not reachable.
- **Support:** active. Lancair Aerospace lists service bulletins for the IV,
  IV-P and IV-PT (for example SB078-0722 Rev B, nose gear drag links) and sells
  parts (lancair.com, P/S).

## Drawings, molds and tooling

- **2017:** inventories, production fixtures and support materials moved from
  Redmond to Garner Field, Uvalde, Texas (company release, P/S).
- **2023:** reported sale to JetEXE of "older kit designs, molds, and
  intellectual property" (AvWeb, S). The purchase agreement is not public, so the
  exact asset list is unconfirmed.
- **Do the IV-P molds survive in usable condition?** Not stated anywhere public.

## Kit or plans rights

- Kit sold by Lancair (Neico Aviation, then Lancair International) from the early
  1990s. Kit production ended in 2012 (Wikipedia, L).
- No IV-P kits are sold today. Lancair Aerospace's current products are the Mako
  and Barracuda.
- Kit rights followed the 2017 and 2023 sales (above). The 2017 release names
  the IV and IV-P explicitly. The 2023 reports say "older kit designs" without a
  model list.

## Patents and trademarks

- **Patents.** No IV-P patent identified. Anything filed before 2006 has expired.
- **"LANCAIR"**, Reg. 7828166 (SN 98225145), Class 12: live, registered 10 June
  2025. Owners of record: Akyna Medical Management, LLC and Jetexe Aviation
  Corporation, both Sacramento. "LANCAIR AEROSPACE", Reg. 7828165, same owners,
  live (USPTO, P).
- **"LANCAIR"**, Reg. 1708005 (Lancair International Inc., Redmond): cancelled 3
  March 2023 (TSDR, P). "LANCAIR 200" (Neico Aviation): dead.
- **"PROPJET":** no mark found. A revival could not use "Lancair".

## Fleet

- FAA registry, ReleasableAircraft database downloaded 2026-10-24. Records whose
  make, model or kit field reads as Lancair IV-P, IVP, 4P, IV-PT or Propjet:
  **235** (232 valid). About 43 of these are Propjet/turbine variants (P).
- Lancair IV, unpressurised or unspecified: a further **58** (P).
- These are lower bounds. Amateur-built records use the builder's name, and some
  will not say "Lancair".
- **Built:** about 360 IV-family aircraft by 2011, 110 IV and 250 IV-P (L).

## Why it died

Lancair stopped the IV-P kit in 2012 and in 2016 decided to sell all its older
lines to concentrate on the Evolution. Evolution then closed in late 2017, citing
product-liability insurance after fatal accidents (AOPA 2016, S; Wikipedia, L).
The IV family's accident record is a real part of the story: the NTSB's 2012
Appleton report said 26% of Lancair airplanes had been in accidents and 19% in
fatal ones (Wikipedia citing NTSB, L). The safety dossier belongs to the next
stage.
