# Cirrus VK-30 — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon (census legwork
delegated, judgement mine). **Board issue:** AER-3787. **Date:** 2026-10-24.
**Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only. Nobody has been
contacted. From AERO's stage 1 long list (pass, speed unconditioned).

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **No type certificate. The VK-30 was a kit only.** FAA DRS has no TCDS for
  "VK-30" or "VK30". The same query shape returns A6SW for M22 and A00009CH for
  SR20 (P).
- **The seller was Cirrus Design, and Cirrus still exists and is large.** Cirrus
  Aircraft Limited listed in Hong Kong in July 2024. CAIGA Hong Kong was to hold
  about 85% after the offering. CAIGA is about 73.39% owned by AVIC (prospectus,
  P). So the original seller is a live, state-controlled company, not an orphan.
- **No public record says who holds VK-30 drawings, molds or tooling.** The
  prospectus names the VK-30 only as company history. Its intellectual-property
  section does not mention it (P). The default assumption is that whatever
  survives belongs to the Cirrus group. That is an assumption, not a finding.
- **Cirrus stepped back from the type itself.** It stopped kit sales in 1993 for
  the SR20. It announced a stronger replacement wing for about 28 delivered kits
  in 1996. In 2005 it gave its demonstrator to the EAA museum (S/L).
- **The fleet is almost gone:** 2 aircraft on the FAA register, 1 of them in the
  EAA museum. 12 N-numbers have been deregistered (P).
- **The name:** "CIRRUS" is a live Cirrus Design mark for aircraft. No "VK-30"
  mark was found (P).
- **No successor design.** The ST-50 (built for Israviation, two built) is a
  different aircraft. The SR20 replaced the VK-30 in the business, not in the
  design (L).

## Holder or seller today

- **Original seller:** Cirrus Design, founded by Alan and Dale Klapmeier. The
  prospectus says it was set up in 1987, the VK-30 first flew in 1988, and the
  company moved to Duluth in 1994 (P). Other sources say 1984 (L).
- **Ownership chain** (prospectus, P): Klapmeier family and investors; Arcapita
  majority before 2011; acquired by CAIGA in June 2011; Cirrus Aircraft Limited
  (Cayman) listed on HKEX, stock code 2507, dealings from 12 July 2024.
- **Controlling shareholders:** AVIC, CAIGA and CAIGA Hong Kong. CAIGA Hong Kong
  about 85.0% after the offering; CAIGA about 73.39% owned by AVIC (P).
- **Support for the VK-30:** none found. No VK-30 publications, parts or service
  bulletins on any Cirrus page found. Kit sales stopped in 1993 (L). The 1996
  replacement-wing plan is reported, but whether wings were delivered is not
  known (L).

## Drawings, molds and tooling

- **Unknown.** No public statement on whether VK-30 molds or tooling survive.
  A search for reports of their destruction found nothing either way.
- The 2024 prospectus describes Cirrus's IP as trademarks, patents and know-how
  for the current products. It does not list VK-30 assets (P).
- **Physical survivors:** N33VK, serial 5, the factory demonstrator and "the
  first production prototype upon which all kits were based". Cirrus gave it to
  the EAA museum in 2005. The FAA registry shows EAA Aviation Foundation as owner
  (EAA museum page, S; FAA registry, P). One private aircraft, N947TP, is
  registered with no airworthiness date (P).

## Kit or plans rights

- Cirrus Design sold kits from about 1987-88 to 1993. About 40 kits were
  delivered and 4 factory prototypes built. 13 customer aircraft were completed
  (Wikipedia, L; EAA museum says about 13 built and flown, S).
- Kits are not sold. No plans were ever sold separately.
- **Whether kit rights passed through the 2011 merger to today's group** is not
  shown in any public record. Nothing suggests they were sold elsewhere.

## Patents and trademarks

- **Patents.** No VK-30 patent was identified. Any patent filed in the 1980s
  has expired.
- **"CIRRUS"**, Reg. 1907261, Class 12: live, owned by Cirrus Design Corporation,
  Duluth MN, renewed July 2025 (USPTO TSDR, P). Other live Cirrus Class 12 marks
  exist (CIRRUS VISION, CIRRUS PERCEPTION, Reg. 4651692).
- **"VK-30" / "VK30":** no USPTO marks found (P). Positive control: the same
  search for "CIRRUS" returns the Cirrus marks above.
- A revival could not use the Cirrus name.

## Fleet

- FAA registry, ReleasableAircraft database downloaded 2026-10-24, all records
  matching "VK-30/VK 30/VK30" in make, model or kit fields: **2 aircraft** (P).
  - N33VK, Cirrus VK 30, 1991, experimental amateur-built, owner EAA Aviation
    Foundation Inc. (museum).
  - N947TP, kit "Cirrus Design Corp / Cirrus VK-30", no airworthiness date.
- Deregistered file: 12 N-numbers matching VK-30 (P). Some may be re-registrations.
- **Built:** about 13 customer aircraft plus 4 prototypes (L/S). 4 registered in
  2018 (L).

## Why it died

Cirrus stopped selling the kit in 1993 to put everything into the certified SR20.
The founders concluded from the VK-30 that a conventional layout and a certified
production aircraft were the way forward (Wikipedia, L; Plane & Pilot, S). The
1996 plan for a "stronger replacement wing" (Wikipedia, L) points to a wing problem on delivered
kits; that is my reading, not a sourced finding. So the engine was not the only
issue, and a re-engine does not fix the story on its own. That is AERO's call.

|---|---|---|
| **Buy** | Buy whatever VK-30 data and tooling survive from Cirrus Aircraft. | Unlikely. A listed, AVIC-controlled company that markets itself on certified aircraft has little reason to sell a 1993 kit with a wing history. Requires contact; not done. |
| **License** | A licence from Cirrus for drawings or the design. | Same. Cirrus would weigh brand and liability risk. Requires contact; not done. |
| **Modify existing airframes** | Work on the 2 registered airframes. | Not useful. One is a museum piece. |
| **Re-derive from an airframe** | Measure a surviving VK-30 and design anew. | Physical access is the problem: the best example is in a museum. Whether it is lawful depends on unknown data rights. **Attorney needed.** |

## Where an aviation IP attorney is needed

1. **Whether VK-30 design data and tooling passed to Cirrus Aircraft Limited** in
   the 2011 CAIGA merger and the 2024 reorganization, and what rights a
   1980s-90s kit design carries today (copyright in drawings, trade secrets).
2. **Re-derivation from a measured airframe**, given an identifiable, active
   rights holder.
3. **Terms of any approach** to Cirrus, if Bill authorises one. Export-control and
   foreign-ownership questions may arise because the group is AVIC-controlled.
