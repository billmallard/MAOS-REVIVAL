# Cozy MKIV — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon (census legwork
delegated, judgement mine). **Board issue:** AER-3787. **Date:** 2026-10-24.
**Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only. Nobody has been
contacted. From AERO's stage 1 long list (the survey's reference design; near
miss: range 951 nm, 49 nm short).

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **Aircraft Spruce & Specialty Co. owns the Cozy MKIV plans.** It says it
  bought the designs from Co-Z Development and has had "the exclusive right to
  market these designs" since 1 January 2004 (Aircraft Spruce product page; P,
  the holder's own statement).
- **It claims US copyright and says it licenses nobody else.** "These designs
  are protected with U.S. copyrights, and we have licensed no one else to copy
  all or any part of these designs to sell, loan, or give to others" (same, P;
  quote corrected 2026-10-24 to the page's exact wording).
- **Plans are on sale: $500, to builders only.** Each buyer signs a licence to
  build one airplane. The licence also bars using the drawings to design
  "another aircraft" (Aircraft Spruce licence form, P).
- **Aircraft Spruce gives no technical support.** The licence says so. Support
  comes from builder groups and from Marc Zeitlin (Burnside Aerospace), who is
  unofficial (P).
- **The Rutan lineage is licensed.** Nat Puffer derived the Cozy from the
  Long-EZ with Rutan's approval. He had a licensing agreement with Rutan and
  RAF and paid a royalty on each plans set. Rutan later "tried to cancel" it
  (AOPA Pilot, December 1994; S). The deal dates from before RAF stopped
  licensing in June 1985. Whether it passed to Aircraft Spruce, and whether it
  still runs, is **not public.**
- **No COZY aircraft trademark exists** (USPTO, P).
- **Fleet:** 124 registrations read as a MKIV and 195 Cozys of all marks (FAA
  registry, P). About 350 MKIVs built by 2015 (Wikipedia, L).

## Holder or seller today

- **Aircraft Spruce & Specialty Co.** (Corona, California) sells the plans
  (part 01-00178, $500) and parts. Plans "can only be sold to a builder";
  buyers must give a plans number for drawings (P).
- **Co-Z Development** (Nat Puffer's company) was the designer and first seller.
  It appears as manufacturer on two registered MKIVs, N14CZ and N44CZ (1988)
  (FAA registry, P). Its corporate status today was not checked. The Arizona
  record was not reached.
- **Nat Puffer.** A Star Tribune obituary lists a Nathan Puffer who died on
  19 August 2022 (L, search-result snippet only; the page returned 403). It is
  **not confirmed** that this is the Cozy designer. The licence form protects
  the designers' "heirs or assigns" and "trusts", which fits an estate plan
  but proves nothing (P).
- **Support:** builder groups since 2004; Marc Zeitlin unofficially (P).

## Drawings, molds and tooling

- **Plans-built, moldless construction.** There are no factory molds to inherit.
  Some prefabricated parts come from third-party suppliers through Aircraft
  Spruce.
- **The drawings are Aircraft Spruce's.** The licence says the drawings,
  instructions and manuals "remain the property of Aircraft Spruce" (P).
- **The prototype:** not traced. The two Co-Z-built aircraft (N14CZ, N44CZ) are
  now owned by private persons (FAA registry, P).

## Kit or plans rights

- **Aircraft Spruce licence terms** (licence form, P). The builder agrees:
  - to build one airplane only, conforming to the drawings;
  - not to let anyone else use the drawings for a second airplane or part;
  - not to transfer the drawings without Aircraft Spruce's approval;
  - not to copy them;
  - **not to use the drawings in the design or manufacture of another
    aircraft.**
  - Later buyers, heirs and assigns are bound too.
- **Rutan.** Puffer built the first Cozy (three seats, plans ready January 1984)
  under a licensing agreement with Rutan and RAF and paid royalties per set
  (AOPA 1994, S). Wikipedia says "with approval from Burt Rutan" (L). RAF's
  1985 notice says it "cannot issue further licenses" (Canard Pusher 54, S).
  That covers new licences, not Puffer's existing one, as far as the text
  goes.

## Patents and trademarks

- **Patents.** None identified. Any patent from the 1980s-90s has expired.
- **"COZY":** about 1,800 USPTO records; none for aircraft. The only aviation
  hits are travel blankets and software (USPTO search index, P).
- Positive control: the same search for "MOONEY" returns live SN 72336652,
  aircraft and parts (P).
- The name is unregistered for aircraft, but Aircraft Spruce has used it since
  2004 and Co-Z since the 1980s. **Attorney needed** before any use.

## Fleet

- FAA registry, ReleasableAircraft database downloaded 2026-10-24 (P):
  - **124** registrations whose name reads as a MKIV ("MK IV", "Mark 4",
    "Cozy IV" and similar).
  - **195** with "COZY" anywhere, which includes Cozy IIIs and unlabelled
    entries.
  - Builder-entered names, so the counts are approximate. Positive control:
    the same method returns 14 for the Mooney M22.
- **Built:** about 350 MKIVs by 2015 (Wikipedia, L). Plans sales numbers are
  not public.
- First-flight date conflicts: 1988 (Wikipedia, Cozy III article) or 1993
  (Wikipedia, MKIV article) (L). The registry has a 1988 Co-Z-built MKIV (P).

## Why it died

It has not. Plans and parts are still sold by Aircraft Spruce, which bought
the design in 2004 (P). What stopped is factory support: Aircraft Spruce
provides none, and builders support each other.

## The paths

| Path | What it would take | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Buy** | Buy the Cozy designs from Aircraft Spruce. | Possible in principle. Aircraft Spruce is a large, stable firm, and the Cozy is a sideline for it. Requires contact; not done. |
| **License** | A licence from Aircraft Spruce for an Aeromomentum-powered Cozy, or a firewall-forward package. Check whether the Rutan agreement passed to Aircraft Spruce. | The most natural path for the reference design. Aircraft Spruce says it has licensed nobody so far. Requires contact. |
| **Modify existing airframes** | Fit Aeromomentum engines to new or existing Cozys. Each builder is the manufacturer and has his own one-airplane licence. | Normal homebuilt practice for one airplane. Selling a package is a separate question. **Attorney needed** on the package. |
| **Re-derive from an airframe** | Measure a Cozy and build a new design. | The plans licence bars using the drawings in "another aircraft". Anyone who has signed it is bound. **Attorney needed.** |

## Where an aviation IP attorney is needed

1. **Scope of Aircraft Spruce's copyright** and of clause E of the plans
   licence (no use in another aircraft's design), and whether a measured, not
   copied, derivative is caught.
2. **The Puffer-Rutan agreement:** did it pass to Aircraft Spruce in 2004, is it
   still in force, and does Rutan (or whoever holds the RAF rights) have any
   claim on a Cozy derivative?
3. **The COZY name** and any unregistered rights in it.
4. **Terms of any licence or purchase**, if Bill authorises an approach.
