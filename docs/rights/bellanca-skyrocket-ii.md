# Bellanca 19-25 Skyrocket II — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon (census legwork
delegated, judgement mine). **Board issue:** AER-3787. **Date:** 2026-10-24.
**Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only. Nobody has been
contacted. From AERO's stage 1 long list (pass).

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **There is no type certificate.** No FAA TCDS exists for the 19-25 or
  Skyrocket II. The absence check was run with a positive control (FAA DRS, P).
  No public record of a TC application was found.
- **The one prototype survives, in a museum.** N771AB, s/n 001, built 1974, is
  registered to Friends of Bellanca Airfield Inc., New Castle, Delaware, since
  April 2021. Its certificate is experimental exhibition (FAA registry, P). The
  museum says the previous owners donated it in 2020 (museum newsletter, S).
- **The design's owner is not known.** August Bellanca developed it through
  Bellanca Aircraft Engineering Inc. and later AviaBellanca Inc., of which he
  was president when he died in March 2010 (S). No public record shows where his
  design rights went. The museum got the airframe, not necessarily any rights.
- **A 1996-98 kit and certification revival failed.** AviaBellanca of Reston,
  Virginia announced a Skyrocket III kit and a Part 23 TC for 1999. It needed
  about $7 million and did not get it (Flight International, S).
- **The airframe could be measured, carefully.** The wings are molded into the
  fuselage and cannot be removed without cutting (museum, S).
- **The "Bellanca" name is separate.** The old BELLANCA registration (Reg.
  1066673) was cancelled in 2020. Bellanca Aircraft Inc. now holds the Viking TCs
  but has no stated link to the Skyrocket II (P).

**None: never certified.** The museum says plans to certify and build 100 a year
were never achieved (S). In 1983 August Bellanca put the cost of certification at
$20-25 million (Air & Space, citing Flying, S). The aircraft was "designed and
built to comply with FAA requirements for production aircraft" (General Aviation
News obituary, S). No FAA record of an application was found. An application
that never led to a TC would not appear in DRS anyway.

## Holder or seller today

- **Bellanca Aircraft Engineering Inc.**, Scott Depot, West Virginia: August
  Bellanca's company for the project (Wikipedia, L). The registry's model record
  names "Bellanca Aircraft Eng. Inc." as the builder (P). Status unknown.
- **The West Virginia investor group** backed the project from 1971. It fell out
  with Bellanca over how to build the aircraft and who ran the company, and the
  project stopped before certification flight testing (secondary sources quoted
  in search results; original article not read, S/L).
- **AviaBellanca Inc.**, Reston, Virginia: led by August Bellanca until his
  death on 16 March 2010 (General Aviation News, S). Status unknown. Virginia SCC
  records were not searched.
- **The estate.** He was survived by his wife, two sons and a daughter (S). A
  son, August J. Bellanca, posted publicly about the aircraft in 2023 (forum, L).
  Whether any of them hold rights is unknown.
- **"Skyrocket Inc."** (named in the brief): no record of a company by this name
  connected to the 19-25 was found.
- **Friends of Bellanca Airfield Inc.**, a Delaware museum, is the registered
  owner of the airframe (P).
- **Support:** none.

## Drawings, molds and tooling

- **Payne Engineering** of Princeton built the prototype, completed in 1975
  (Air & Space, S). Its records were not traced.
- **No public statement** on whether drawings, molds or the 1990s kit tooling
  survive. The museum's 2020 note mentions only the airframe (S).
- **NASA flight-test data** is public: NASA/Ohio State measured CD0 = 0.016 (NTRS
  19770054238, per the long list), and NASA Langley ran laminar-flow tests,
  reported in a 1984 NASA technical paper (S).
- The Smithsonian holds Giuseppe Bellanca's archives (donated by August Bellanca
  in 1993). Whether they include 19-25 material is unknown.

**Status: unknown**, except that the airframe exists.

## Kit or plans rights

- **Skyrocket III kit, 1996-98.** AviaBellanca offered kits at $85,000 (basic) or
  $97,000 (fast-build), and pressurized versions at $102,000-118,000. Build time
  was estimated at 3,500 hours (Flight International, S; forum, L).
- **Deliveries:** Wikipedia says 11 kits had been delivered by 1998 (L). A forum
  says there is no evidence any were delivered (L). No Skyrocket III appears in
  the FAA registry under the model searched. Unresolved.
- No plans or kits are sold today.

## Patents and trademarks

- **Patents.** August Bellanca held two patents (GA News, S). They were not
  identified. Any patent from his 1966 construction method has expired.
- **"BELLANCA"**, Reg. 1066673, Class 12, last owner Alexandria Aircraft LLC:
  **cancelled** 16 March 2020 (USPTO TSDR, P). Whether Bellanca Aircraft Inc.
  has filed a new mark was not checked.
- **"SKYROCKET" for aircraft:** "RILEY SKYROCKET" and "RILEY SUPER SKYROCKET"
  (aircraft modification services, Superskyrocket LLC) are both cancelled (TSDR,
  P). A Class 28 SKYROCKET mark (toys) exists (L). No live Class 12 SKYROCKET mark
  was found, but the search was not complete.

## Fleet

- FAA registry, make BELLANCA model 19-25, 2026-10-24: **1 aircraft** (P).
  N771AB, s/n 001, year 1974, Friends of Bellanca Airfield Inc., New Castle DE,
  registered 9 April 2021, valid to April 2028, certificate experimental
  exhibition, airworthiness date 26 June 1997. The model record is coded
  amateur-built (P).
- **Built:** 1. It last flew from Summit Airport to Georgetown, Delaware, then
  sat in storage for about 10 years before moving to the museum in 2020 (museum,
  S). Not airworthy now: the engine and tail surfaces were removed for the move.

## Why it died

The founder and the West Virginia investors fell out over how to run the
programme, and it stopped before certification flight testing (S/L). The early
1980s collapse of the GA market ended production plans (Wikipedia, L). The
1990s revival could not raise the money to certify, and the museum says the
project ended when August Bellanca died in 2010 (S).
