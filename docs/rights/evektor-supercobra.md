# Evektor VUT100-131i SuperCobra — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon (census legwork
delegated, judgement mine). **Board issue:** AER-3787. **Date:** 2026-10-24.
**Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only. Nobody has been
contacted. From AERO's stage 1 long list (pass, speed unconditioned).

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **No TC exists, at EASA or the FAA.** Neither the EASA TCDS listing nor FAA
  DRS has a VUT100, Cobra or SuperCobra record. Positive controls (other Evektor
  types) return hits in both (P).
- **The design is still on the maker's website.** Evektor's 2026 site lists the
  "SuperCobra" with both versions and calls the VUT-120i an "Aircraft
  Prototype" (P). So Evektor has not publicly given the design up.
- **It was not designed by Evektor alone.** The VUT100 was designed by the
  Institute of Aerospace Engineering at Brno University of Technology (BUT),
  under a Czech Ministry of Industry and Trade grant (FI-IM/038). Evektor was the
  "future producer" and DOA holder (S, Píštěk and Popela 2007; BUT site). Rights
  may be split between BUT, Evektor and the grant terms.
- **The corporate picture is messy.** The company named in the press,
  EVEKTOR-AEROTECHNIK a.s., is still registered but now has a Bradlec address,
  far from Kunovice. It had an insolvency petition in July 2015 that ended within
  weeks. The aircraft business now trades from Kunovice as EVEKTOR, spol. s r.o.
  and Evektor Aircraft a.s. (P, Czech ARES register).
- **At least one VUT100-family aircraft flew privately until 2021.** OK-MIH, a
  "VUT100-120IX Cobra VPR", crashed in Slovakia on 27 August 2021, killing 3 (S,
  ASN, citing the Slovak investigation). The SuperCobra prototype's fate is unknown.
- **Development slowed in June 2007 for lack of money** (S, Flight). The
  SuperCobra first flew in October/November 2006, aiming for EASA/FAA
  certification in early 2008 (S, FLYER).

## Drawings, molds and tooling

- **Unknown.** The airframe is all-metal (duralumin), so tooling is jigs rather
  than molds (P, Evektor site).
- BUT's institute says the work ran 2004-2006 under the grant (P, BUT site). It
  may hold design records.
- Prototypes: the SuperCobra first flight was reported in November 2006 (S). The
  number of airframes built and where they are now was not found.

## Kit or plans rights

None. It was meant as a factory-built certified aircraft.

- **Patents.** None found. Not searched in Czech or EPO databases.
- **"EVEKTOR"**, SN 79034149 (Evektor, spol. s r.o.), class 12: **abandoned**
  (USPTO trademark search, P).
- **"SUPERCOBRA"**, SN 76162691 (Bell Helicopter Textron), class 12 helicopters:

  aircraft mark, SN 76436781 (P).

## Fleet

- FAA registry, make EVEKTOR, 2026-10-24: only SportStar and Harmony models. **No
  VUT100 or Cobra** (P). Control: MOONEY M22 returns 14 (P).
- **Built:** unknown; a handful of prototypes. OK-MIH (MSN 2010 0101) was
  destroyed in 2021 (S).

## Why it died

Evektor slowed both the EV-55 Outback and the VUT100 in June 2007 for funding
reasons (Flight International, 14 June 2007; S, headline and lead only). The
company put its resources into the light-sport SportStar and Harmony and into
engineering services. Certification, planned for early 2008, never happened (S,
FLYER 2006).
