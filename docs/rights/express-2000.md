# Express (Wheeler Express / Series 90 / Express 2000 FT) — rights and provenance

**Stage:** 2, rights and provenance. **Researcher:** Elon (census legwork
delegated, judgement mine). **Board issue:** AER-3787. **Date:** 2026-10-24.
**Status:** first pass, desk research from public sources only. Nobody has been
contacted. From AERO's stage 1 long list (near miss: useful load 99 lb short).

This is research, not legal advice. It reports what public records show and
where they run out. It does not say the design may be copied.

Source quality: **P** primary or official record; **S** reputable secondary;
**L** low (wiki, forum, aggregator).

## Summary

- **No type certificate. The Express was always a kit.** FAA DRS returns nothing
  for "Express" (P).
- **The design has passed through at least five owners, three of them through
  bankruptcy:**
  1. Wheeler Technology (Ken Wheeler): first flight 1987,
     bankrupt about two years after the 1990 crash.
  2. Express Design, Inc. (Redmond, OR): bought the assets; Chapter 7 on 6
     January 1996.
  3. Express Aircraft Company, LLC (Olympia, WA, Paul Fagerstrom, from about
     1997): bought plans rights, tooling, jigs, inventory and engineering records.
  4. Exosphere Aircraft Company, Inc. (WA): took the membership interest in EAC on
     5 July 2005.
  5. Composite Aircraft Technology, LLC (CAT, Toledo, WA, president Darrell
     Peterson): bought "all assets of the former EAC through bankruptcy court" in
     September 2007.
  (Wikipedia, L; Kitplanes, S; EAA, S; forum copy of the 2005 release, L.)
- **CAT is the last known holder.** There is no public sign of it after about
  2009-2010. Its web domain (compairtechllc.com) now serves an unrelated Chinese
  film site (checked 2026-10-24). Washington SOS records were not reachable, so
  its corporate status is **unknown**.
- **No live trademark.** Express Design's "EXPRESS" mark (Reg. 1822621) was
  cancelled in 2001 (P).
- **Fleet:** at least 44 on the FAA register (P). Kitplanes says about 100 were
  registered and about 55 are still active (S). More than 300 Express 2000 kits
  were delivered (L).
- **This is the most orphan-like file in my set.** The trail ends at a small
  company with no visible presence. Whether anyone can sell or license the design
  is the key open fact.

## Holder or seller today

- **Composite Aircraft Technology, LLC**, 148 Skyhawk Drive, Toledo, WA 98591
  (EAA kit directory, S). In 2009 Peterson said CAT had spent two years getting the
  kit back into production and supplying parts to earlier customers: "We are a
  different company and we are here to stay" (General Aviation News 2009, via
  search excerpt, S).
- **Status today: unknown.** No news after about 2010 was found. The domain has
  lapsed to an unrelated site. Washington SOS (CCFS) and its open-data copies
  could not be queried from here.
- **Express Aircraft Company, LLC** and **Express Design, Inc.** are both
  bankrupt predecessors. Neither appears in Oregon's active-business data (P).
  Express Aircraft Company was a Washington company; not checked.
- **Support:** builder self-help. A newsletter, "The Express Link", has served
  builders since 1992 (EAA, S). No current factory support found.

## Drawings, molds and tooling

- **The assets moved as a package at each sale:** to EDI after Wheeler's
  bankruptcy; to EAC with "plans, machine tools, jigs, inventory, engineering
  records"; to CAT in 2007 as "all assets" (Wikipedia, L; GA News, S).
- CAT redesigned the tail (larger area, no cruciform) (Kitplanes, S). So CAT
  probably held working molds as of about 2009. That is an inference.
- **Where the molds are now, and whether they survive, is unknown.**

## Kit or plans rights

- Variants: Wheeler Express (FT, CT), EDI Express, Express Series 90, Series 2000
  (FT, RG, ER), Millennium, and a PT6 Express 2000RG (Wikipedia, L; Flight, S;
  FAA registry kit fields, P).
- No kit is on sale today that I could find.
- **Who holds the kit rights today is not established.** If CAT has dissolved,
  its assets may have been sold, abandoned or left with its members. Only state
  and court records can show that.

## Patents and trademarks

- **Patents.** None identified. Anything from the 1987-2007 period has expired
  or will within months (20 years from filing).
- **"EXPRESS"**, Reg. 1822621, Class 12, Express Design, Inc., Redmond OR:
  registered February 1994, **cancelled 4 March 2001** (USPTO TSDR, P).
- **"WHEELER EXPRESS"**, **"EXPRESS AIRCRAFT"**, **"EXPRESS 2000"** (aircraft):
  no Class 12 marks found (P). Positive control: the same search for "EXPRESS"
  returns the cancelled Express Design mark and unrelated live marks.
- "EXPRESS" alone is used by others in Class 12 (for example Turning Point
  Propellers, live), so a revival would want a new name anyway.

## Fleet

- FAA registry, ReleasableAircraft database downloaded 2026-10-24. Records whose
  model or kit field reads as Wheeler Express, Express FT/CT, Series 90, Series
  2000, Millennium or EX2K: **44** (43 valid, 1 registration pending) (P).
  - Kit makers recorded: Wheeler Aircraft Co, Express Aircraft Co LLC, Express
    Aircraft Inc.
  - Newest airworthiness date seen: 2017 (N519EA).
- This is a lower bound. Builder-named records that omit "Express" are missed.
- **Built:** about 100 registered over time, about 55 active (Kitplanes, S). More
  than 300 Express 2000 kits delivered (L), so many kits were never finished.

## Why it died

The second Wheeler demonstrator crashed on the way to Oshkosh in 1990, killing
four. The NTSB blamed the pilot. A later CAFE Foundation evaluation (1997) pointed
to poor yaw stability and weak low-speed control. Wheeler Technology "never really
recovered" and went bankrupt about two years later. Each later owner ran short of
money or customers (Kitplanes, S; CAFE report, S). A 2015 NTSB report again cited
the cruciform tail's yaw stability (Wikipedia, L). The tail, not the engine, is
the design issue to solve.

## The paths

| Path | What it would take | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Buy** | Find CAT or its successor and buy the molds, data and kit rights. | Possibly cheap, if anyone can sell. First the holder has to be found. Requires public-records work, then contact; not done. |
| **License** | Licence from whoever holds the assets. | Same. A licence from a defunct company is worth little without clear title. |
| **Modify existing airframes** | Work with the ~44-55 active airframes. | Upgrades only. Not production. |
| **Re-derive from an airframe** | Measure an Express and design anew, with a new tail. | Physically easy: many airframes, and the CAFE report gives good data. Whether it is lawful depends on rights nobody has yet traced. **Attorney needed.** |

## Where an aviation IP attorney is needed

1. **Who holds the Express design, drawings and molds today**: CAT, its members,
   a buyer, or nobody. This needs Washington SOS and bankruptcy court records.
2. **What survives a chain of three bankruptcies**: what passed at each sale, and
   whether any rights lapsed or were abandoned.
3. **Re-derivation from a measured airframe**, especially if the holder cannot be
   found.
