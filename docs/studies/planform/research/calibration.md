# AER-3807 calibration set: low-aspect-ratio and pusher GA aircraft

Desk research on the public web only, 2026-10-24. Nobody was contacted.

Grades: **F** = factory or designer claim, **P** = flight test or magazine pilot report, **B** = builder or owner, **E** = estimate (whose is stated). "WP" means a Wikipedia infobox. Its grade is the grade of the source the infobox cites, and that source was not re-read unless stated. **AR(d)** means I derived the aspect ratio myself as b²/S. "nf" means not found.

## Part B: The low-aspect-ratio school

### Who argues for low AR

| Who | Where | Claim | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barnaby Wainfan (Northrop Grumman aerodynamicist; wrote Kitplanes "Wind Tunnel" for 38 years, last column Sept 2025) | PAV report (2004), https://wainfan.co/media/pavreport.pdf | AR "between approximately 1.0 and 2.5" all-lifting body. Claims: structural efficiency from short, deep structure; lower parts count; flat-panel facets suit CNC; no classical stall, with vortex lift at high AoA; lower wing loading gives slow landing; wide CG range. Says such an airplane "can cost up to 50% less" than a conventional one. Study airplane matches a C152 on 80 hp versus 100 hp | E (designer study, partly from FMX-4 flight data) |
| Wainfan | PAV section 5 (transport efficiency) | Admits the C150 has higher L/D than the FMX-4 at every speed, and concludes: "In essence, the higher structural efficiency of the low aspect ratio configuration is just sufficient to overcome its aerodynamic disadvantage relative to the conventional airplane." | E |
| Wainfan | PAV eq. 6.4.1.1 | Useful-load-fraction fit for low-AR airplanes: Wu/Wg = 0.5484 + 0.0081 AR² − 0.0551 AR, fitted to FMX-4, Hatfield, Dyke and Arup data. Gives about 0.47-0.50 for AR 1-3 | E (Wainfan; small sample, author notes "considerable scatter") |
| Wainfan | wainfan.co BatRay section | Lists "high structural efficiency, inherent stall resistance, excellent visibility, and outstanding payload fraction" for the configuration | F |

No company is currently selling a low-AR GA pusher that I found. Today the "school" is in practice Wainfan (the BatRay build, NASA report and annual talks) plus Verhees in Belgium (deltas, tractor) and a legacy line: Zimmerman/V-173, Snyder/Hoffman Arup, Hatfield, Dyke. Wainfan says that material carrying his byline on kitplanes.com after September 2025 is recycled without his consent (https://barnabywainfan.substack.com/p/a-new-home-for-my-writing-and-batray). I found no Kitplanes "Wind Tunnel" column devoted specifically to low AR. I scanned his Kitplanes author index (https://www.kitplanes.com/author/barnaby-wainfan/), but many titles are just "Wind Tunnel", so a low-AR column may exist and not be identifiable from the index.

### Counter-arguments

| Who | Where | Point |
|---|---|---|
| Wainfan himself | "Design Process: Span and Aspect Ratio", Kitplanes, 14 May 2020, https://www.kitplanes.com/design-process-span-and-aspect-ratio/ | Induced drag depends on span, not AR: "all wings of the same span will generate the same amount of induced drag regardless of wing chord, area or aspect ratio". He cites Kohlman (J. Aircraft 16(4), 1979) on a NASA advanced-technology light airplane whose span cut cancelled its gains in climb and at altitude. He also notes that "a lot of standard T-hangars are 38 feet wide", so span above ~37 ft costs hangar money. That gives a span cap without implying low AR. |
| de Piolenc (Rohr) | dP | "Deltas need high static thrust for takeoff, when their high induced drag puts them at a disadvantage". The Rohr needed a 4,400 rpm shrouded fan to compensate. |
| Bart Verhees | KP-VD2 | "The delta wing is not so safe when it comes to stalls." Stall can start between half span and tip, giving wing drop and spin; the D2 counters this with a more elliptical planform. The article (Phelps's text, not a Verhees quote) says flaps are not an option on the planform, so the wing must be larger, which adds induced drag. |
| PAV report | PAV section 5 | The L/D deficit is real; the structural gain only breaks even on transport efficiency. PAV section 6.5 says lightweight panels may not resist "hangar rash". |
| mstull, HBA | https://homebuiltairplanes.com/threads/wing-aspect-ratio.4913/ | Tip losses and thick boundary layers on low-AR wings; argues higher AR is more aerodynamically efficient. |
| BoKu, Vigilant1, HBA | https://homebuiltairplanes.com/threads/high-aspect-ratio-wings.33121/ | Balanced view: span costs spar weight, flutter margin and "larger hangar and tiedown spaces", but high AR's benefit shrinks as speed rises. |
| Audience at Wainfan's 2026 talk | Comment by Archie Cobbs, https://barnabywainfan.substack.com/p/airventure-2026-forums-and-talk | Open questions: gust and turbulence ride at low wing loading; limits on speed and altitude. |
| nestofdragons / Serge Krauss | as above | L/D is low except at low speed; high landing attitude; poor downward view. |

## Biggest gaps

1. **Rohr 2-175:** wing area, root and tip chord, LE sweep, empty weight, cruise and stall speeds are all nf, and the span conflicts (20 ft per dP vs 30 ft per AFro). The dimensions likely survive only in the Fraser book and the TWITT 3-view.
2. **LE sweep** was not found for any of the subjects.
3. **Dyke Delta** chords and sweep are nf. Its stall figure (70-75 mph) rests on WP; I could not confirm it in the A&S text.
4. **Cozy MKIV:** two official sites disagree on canard span and total area. The CAFE report (Sport Aviation, April 1999) was not found online.
5. **Prescott span** differs between the designer (29'4") and WP (28'5"). Prefer the designer figure.
6. **Wainfan's 2025 low-AR talk** (video) was not transcribed.
